Half Fare Card — Major Price Jump
Halbtax — 1-month card for 50% off all rail, bus, boat
The Half Fare Card increased significantly for 2026. The book lists CHF 120; the current price is CHF 150. This is a 25% jump after years of stability. The card still gives 50% off all SBB trains, PostBus, lake boats, and most mountain railways for one month. Despite the increase, it remains the best option for travelers staying 2+ weeks with moderate travel intensity or those based in one region who rent a car for some legs.

Pass Coverage Update
Swiss Travel Pass — free mountains (not just 50% off)
The book states the Swiss Travel Pass provides a 50% discount on most mountain railways. This is still true for most peaks — but five excursions are now fully included (free) with the Swiss Travel Pass in 2026, not just discounted:

• Rigi (1,797m above Lucerne — the Queen of the Mountains)
• Stoos (1,300m, Central Switzerland)
• Stanserhorn (1,898m, above Lake Lucerne)
• Klewenalp (1,590m, above Lake Lucerne — family-friendly plateau with views of the lake, confirmed August 2026)
• Brunni (above Engelberg — view of Titlis, confirmed August 2026)

All other major peaks (Jungfraujoch, Gornergrat, Schilthorn, Pilatus, Titlis) remain at 50% discount, not free. Always verify the current discount at the ticket window or mountain railway website before purchasing.
Practical implication A full-day Rigi excursion from Lucerne now costs CHF 0 with the Swiss Travel Pass (round trip was CHF 90 without). This materially changes the value calculation for 3–4 day passes based in Lucerne.
Scenic Train Reservations — Price Correction
Glacier Express — seat reservation, Zermatt–St. Moritz
The mandatory seat reservation fee is now a flat CHF 54 for both 1st and 2nd class, year-round — it no longer varies by season. The reservation is separate from the ticket price (the base fare is covered for Swiss Travel Pass holders). Optional onboard lunch runs CHF 36 (dish of the day) to CHF 49 (3-course menu of the day).
CHF 49–69 (seasonal, earlier data) → CHF 54 flat (2026) glacierexpress.ch
Bernina Express — seat reservation, Chur–Tirano
The seat reservation supplement for the full Chur–Tirano route increased to CHF 40 (low season) to CHF 44 (high season, 2 May–25 October) — well above the CHF 14–26 range reported in earlier editions. The shorter St. Moritz–Tirano segment is CHF 32 flat. Regular regional trains on the same tracks still require no supplement.

New for 2026
Added August 2026
Jungfraujoch seat reservation — now mandatory in summer
From 1 May to 31 October 2026, seat reservations are compulsory for the Jungfraujoch trains in both directions. Cost: CHF 10 per person (round trip). Book at jungfrau.ch or swissrailways.com at the same time you buy your ticket. Arriving without a reservation during this period means joining an unguaranteed standby queue, which during peak days can mean missing the first available connection entirely.
Rigi — now free with Swiss Travel Pass
The Rigi (the Queen of the Mountains above Lake Lucerne) was previously at 50% discount with the Swiss Travel Pass. In 2026, the round trip from Lucerne is fully included at no extra cost. This is one of the most scenic half-day excursions in Switzerland — sunrise from the summit in summer is justifiably famous. Previously CHF 90 round trip without discounts. Now CHF 0 with the Swiss Travel Pass. If you are based in Lucerne for 2–3 days, this materially changes the value case for buying a pass.
Swiss Travel Pass — Half Fare Card break-even recalculation
With the Half Fare Card now at CHF 150 (up from CHF 120), the break-even calculation changes. The old rule was: if you spend more than CHF 240 on tickets at full price, the Half Fare Card saves money. The new rule: you need to spend more than CHF 300 in full-price tickets for the Half Fare Card to break even. For 3–4 day trips with heavy travel, the Swiss Travel Pass increasingly makes more sense relative to the Half Fare Card than it did in 2025.
GoldenPass Express — no supplement with Swiss Travel Pass
The GoldenPass Express (the direct train from Interlaken to Montreux via the new tilt-axle gauge-change technology) is fully covered by the Swiss Travel Pass with no supplement, unlike the Glacier Express and Bernina Express. A reservation is recommended (CHF 19) but the base fare requires no extra payment on top of your pass. This makes the GoldenPass the most practical scenic route for Swiss Travel Pass holders.
Jungfraujoch — weather check before booking
The mandatory reservation system means you are now committing to a specific date before you know the weather. Check the Jungfraujoch webcam at jungfrau.ch the evening before and morning of your planned visit. If the Sphinx Observatory is hidden at 07:00, conditions rarely improve significantly by midday. The Flex Cancellation Option (available at purchase) costs extra but allows cancellation up to the day before — worth considering for summer visits when afternoon cloud is common.
2027 fares — Swiss Travel Pass and Half Fare Card unaffected
Swiss public transport fares rise by an average of 3.6% from the December 2026 timetable change (confirmed August 2026). This increase applies to season products like the GA Travelcard (2nd class: CHF 4,095, up CHF 100) and standard point-to-point fares. The Swiss Travel Pass and Swiss Half Fare Card — the two passes tourists actually use — are explicitly excluded from this increase. If you are planning travel into 2027, the prices in the table above should hold through at least the December 2026 fare change. Re-check closer to your travel date if booking for late 2027.
Seasonal Alerts
2026 Calendar
!
Cable car closures — April–May and October–November Most cable cars and cogwheel trains outside major year-round operations close for annual maintenance in these windows. The Pilatus cogwheel railway closes mid-November through mid-May. Confirm operating dates for any mountain excursion before building your itinerary. Arriving at a closed lift station wastes half a day and the journey to get there.
!
Peak ski weeks — Christmas–New Year, February school holidays Maximum prices at ski resorts. Hotels in Zermatt, Verbier, St. Moritz, and the Jungfrau region book out months ahead. Lift pass prices at peak. Book 3–6 months ahead or pay a severe premium for last-minute options. Cities (Zurich, Bern, Basel) are quieter during these same weeks.
!
Summer peaks — July–August Maximum crowds at Lucerne, Grindelwald, Interlaken, Zermatt, and all major mountain destinations. Jungfraujoch seat reservations are mandatory in this window. The Glacier Express books out weeks in advance. Hotels reach 90–100% occupancy. Prices reflect the demand. Book accommodation and scenic train reservations 2–3 months ahead.
!
Christmas and New Year — December 24 to January 2 Maximum prices everywhere. Cities trade the low-season discount for holiday premium. Lucerne, Zurich, Montreux, and Bern host large Christmas markets through December 23 — worth visiting. January 2 begins the year low: quietest, cheapest period outside ski resorts.
✓
Best value windows — May–June and September–October Shoulder seasons offer hotels at 20–35% below peak. Hiking conditions are excellent. Mountains are fully operational. Crowds thin noticeably after schools resume in September. The Lavaux vineyards along Lake Geneva are particularly rewarding in October.
✓
Montreux Jazz Festival — early July 2026 The lakefront concerts fill hotels across the Lake Geneva region. Book accommodation early if your dates overlap. Free outdoor performances complement ticketed indoor shows. Hotels sell out months in advance.
✓
Glacier Express reservation window opens 3 months ahead The earliest you can book a Glacier Express seat reservation is 3 months before travel. The first trains of the day in summer (07:00–09:00 from Zermatt or St. Moritz) sell out first. Set a reminder to book as soon as the window opens for summer travel.
