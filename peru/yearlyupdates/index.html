What Changed
Updated August 2026
Major infrastructure — Lima airport
⚠ Lima Airport — now a single unified terminal
Jorge Chávez International Airport’s expanded terminal became fully operational on June 1, 2025, and the old terminal building is now closed to passengers. There is no longer a Terminal 1 / Terminal 2 split — domestic and international flights (including Lima–Cusco) now depart from the same unified building, sharing check-in and arrivals areas before immigration separates flows. No inter-terminal transit is needed. Arrivals processing is on Level 1; check-in and most gates are on Level 3, connected by escalators and elevators. Ride-hailing pickup (Uber/Cabify/DiDi/inDrive) is from the signed Parking E1 zone near the Costa del Sol hotel — walk out from Level 2 and follow the signage; expect a small airport supplement/parking fee added automatically in the app.
Machu Picchu — entry and access
⚠ Machu Picchu: train + entry tickets are separate systems — buy both
Since January 2026, PeruRail and Inca Rail have fully decoupled from the Machu Picchu site entry system, and Inca Trail permits no longer include the Machu Picchu entry ticket either. You must book train tickets (perurail.com or incarail.com) AND Machu Picchu entry circuits (via tuboleto.cultura.pe, linked from machupicchu.gob.pe) as completely separate transactions — and if you’re trekking, a third, separate purchase for the citadel entry itself. Many travellers assume one purchase covers everything — it does not. All three sell out independently and must be secured months in advance in high season.
Machu Picchu — entry fees 2026
The standard circuit entry (Circuits 1–4) is confirmed at S/152 for foreign adult visitors — roughly $45 USD at the current exchange rate (soles has strengthened since earlier in the year, see footer). Combined tickets that include a mountain hike (Huayna Picchu or Machu Picchu Mountain) run S/200 total. Andean Community citizens (Peru, Colombia, Ecuador, Bolivia) and students with valid ID continue to receive discounted rates.

PeruRail — Cusco to Aguas Calientes
Fares on the Ollantaytambo–Aguas Calientes corridor vary significantly by date, season and how far ahead you book. Expedition (economy) one-way fares currently range roughly $35–55, with the lower end available for off-peak dates booked well in advance. Inca Rail’s Voyager remains competitively priced at the lower end. Book directly at perurail.com or incarail.com — it remains the most reliable option for the Machu Picchu corridor.

New budget airline competition on Lima–Cusco
Domestic flights — Lima to Cusco
JetSmart Perú consolidated its Lima–Cusco operations in 2025, adding 4 daily flights and bringing meaningful competition to the LATAM–Sky Airline duopoly. Advance fares for Lima–Cusco are now consistently available from S/89-149 (versus S/200+ previously). The price war has benefited travellers significantly — book 6-8 weeks ahead for best availability.
Typical advance: S/200-300 → Now from: S/89-149 JetSmart / Sky / LATAM
Perú — Lima airport connection fee
Since December 7, 2025, Lima airport charges a new Unified Airport Usage Fee (TUUA) of $11.86 USD — but this applies only to international passengers connecting through Lima to another international flight (i.e. Lima is a stopover, not your final entry or exit point). It is not a general departure tax charged to every visitor. If your airline has an agreement with Lima Airport Partners it may already be included in your ticket; otherwise it is paid online in advance or at the airport. Domestic connections are currently exempt (suspended pending a separate review). Most readers of this guide, flying directly into and out of Lima, will not encounter this fee at all.

Connectivity — the eSIM era in Peru
Peru — mobile connectivity
eSIM technology now works reliably in Peru for most modern smartphones. Airalo (Claro network) and Holafly are the two most reliable eSIM options for Peru, including coverage in Cusco, Aguas Calientes, Paracas, and Arequipa — expect roughly 40-55 Mbps in Lima and 25-30 Mbps in Cusco, plenty for maps, ride-hailing and calls. Activate at home before you travel. Signal in the Amazon and at the Rainbow Mountain trailhead is limited or absent — download maps offline (Google Maps or Maps.me) in Lima or Cusco. Physical SIM: Claro and Movistar are available at the airport — S/30-50 for a tourist plan.
Lima airport — getting picked up
With the airport now operating as a single unified terminal, the ground transport picture is simpler than it used to be, but the pickup zone still trips people up. Uber, Cabify, DiDi and inDrive pickups are from Parking E1, near the Costa del Sol hotel — exit Arrivals on Level 2 and follow the signage across the boulevard; the walk takes a few minutes. Official staffed taxi counters (fixed price by district) are inside both the National and International Arrivals halls. WhatsApp is used extensively by Peruvian transfer operators — your Lima hotel can arrange a named-driver pickup for approximately S/60-95 to Miraflores (more at peak hours) with a WhatsApp confirmation.

Cusco — altitude management
The 3-2-1 rule for Cusco acclimatization: 3 days before any strenuous activity, 2 litres of water per day minimum, 1 alcoholic drink maximum per day (or zero) for the first 48 hours. This was validated in a 2024 study by the Universidad Nacional de San Antonio Abad del Cusco as the most effective non-medication approach for visitors coming from sea level. Coca tea: freely available everywhere in Cusco — it is mild, legal in Peru, and genuinely reduces mild altitude headaches. Note: this specific study reference was not independently re-verified in the August 2026 pass — the general acclimatization advice remains sound regardless.
Lima — food scene update
Two restaurants worth adding to your Lima list for 2026: Merito (Barranco, fermentation-forward, book 3-4 weeks ahead) and Osso Carniceria (Miraflores, exceptional meat, walk-in possible for lunch). For the best ceviche at accessible prices: El Cevichano in Miraflores consistently delivers market-quality fish at S/40-60 per person — not in most guidebooks yet.
Booking ahead — Inca Trail 2027
Inca Trail — 2027 permits
The Ministry of Culture releases Inca Trail permits on a staggered monthly schedule roughly two months ahead of each departure month, with the earliest window opening first. For the 2026 season, that meant permits for January 2026 opened November 17, 2025, with the rest of the year released progressively through mid-January 2026. Following the same pattern, 2027 permits are expected to open in a staggered rollout starting around October–November 2026 — no official date had been published by the Ministry of Culture as of this August 2026 update. If you want Inca Trail dates in the May–September 2027 high season, watch tierrasvivas.com/inca-trail-official-website or a licensed operator’s site closely from September 2026 onward and be ready to book the moment the calendar is published — popular dates can sell out within days.
Seasonal Alerts
2026–2027 calendar
⚠ Inti Raymi (June 24) — Cusco book 3 months ahead
Inti Raymi, the Inca Festival of the Sun, is the largest pre-Columbian celebration still held in the Americas. The ceremony at Sacsayhuamán on June 24 attracts tens of thousands of visitors. All Cusco accommodation books out weeks or months in advance for the June 20-26 window. Train tickets to and from Cusco over this period also sell out. Book accommodation and trains as soon as your dates are set if visiting in June. Stand in the upper section of the Sacsayhuamán amphitheatre for a free view — the paid grandstand seats ($120+) are directly in the sun.
⚠ Rainy season (Jan–Mar) — highland trail closures likely
The Andean rainy season peaks January through March. The Inca Trail closes entirely every February for maintenance. Rainbow Mountain is often snowbound and impassable January–March. The Colca Canyon hike can be dangerous due to landslides and trail washout. If your travel dates fall in this window, focus on Lima, Paracas, and Nazca (coast and desert are unaffected) rather than building a highland-heavy itinerary. The Amazon, paradoxically, is at its most dramatic in high-water season — if Amazon travel is your priority, Jan-May is ideal.
Perú — Holy Week (Semana Santa)
Cusco’s Semana Santa (Holy Week, the week before Easter) is one of the most extraordinary spectacles in South America — centuries-old processions, elaborate floats, candlelit streets. It is also one of the most crowded weeks of the year. Hotels fill months in advance; prices double. If you want to experience it, book accommodation and trains at least 4-5 months ahead. If you are not specifically there for Semana Santa, consider arriving the week after when prices normalise and the experience is calmer.
Perú — Fiestas Patrias (July 28–29)
Perú’s independence days (July 28 and 29) are national public holidays. Most businesses close, public transport is reduced, and Cusco, Lima, and Arequipa city centres are crowded with domestic travellers. Restaurants in tourist areas remain open. Avoid any complex transport connections on these dates — delays and reduced services are common. Machu Picchu and major sites remain open but are at maximum capacity.
Perú — best time to visit
For the classic Peru circuit (Lima, Cusco, Machu Picchu, Sacred Valley): May, September, and October are the sweet spot — dry weather, clear skies at Machu Picchu, post-peak-season pricing, and no trail closures. June-August is busiest (most popular, most expensive, but also most reliable weather). April and November are shoulder season — occasional rain but significantly fewer crowds and better accommodation prices. April in the Sacred Valley is extraordinarily green after the rains.
