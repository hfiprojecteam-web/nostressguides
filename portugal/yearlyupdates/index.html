Sintra — mandatory booking and access changes
Sintra: all major palaces now require advance online booking — no walk-ups
Sintra’s four main palaces — Pena Palace, Moorish Castle, Monserrate, and Quinta da Regaleira — all operate mandatory timed-entry booking. Walk-up tickets are not sold at any of them during April–October. Pena Palace (the most visited) books out 2–3 weeks ahead in July and August. Book at sintrapatrimoniomundial.pt. If you arrive in Sintra without a booking during peak season, you will not enter Pena Palace. Visiting Sintra midweek and booking the moment your travel dates are fixed is the only reliable strategy. The National Palace of Sintra (in the village itself) does accept some walk-ups on weekday mornings in low season.
Sintra — Pena Palace
Pena Palace adult entry is now €14 for the palace exterior and gardens, €20 for full palace including interior. The price increase reflects additional visitor management infrastructure. Saturday and Sunday in July–August: book 3 weeks ahead minimum. Weekdays in May, June, September, October: 1–2 weeks ahead is usually sufficient. Book at sintrapatrimoniomundial.pt. The first slot of the day (09:30) is the least crowded regardless of season.
Gardens €10, interior €14 (book) → €14 / €20 (2026) sintrapatrimoniomundial.pt
Lisbon — tourist tax and transport
Lisbon — tourist tax increased
Lisbon’s municipal tourist tax (taxa turística) increased to €4 per person per night on 1 January 2026, up from €2.50, capped at a maximum of 7 nights (€28 total per person per stay). The tax applies to all paid accommodation in Lisbon municipality — hotel, hostel, Airbnb, or boat hotel. Porto charges €2.50/night (capped at 5 nights). Other Portuguese destinations charge considerably less or nothing. Factor Lisbon’s tax into your accommodation budget.
€2.50/night (book) → €4/night, max 7 nights (2026) lisboaverde.pt
Lisbon — Carris/Metro integrated 24h ticket
The integrated Carris/Metro 24h travel card is now €7.10. This covers unlimited journeys on Lisbon Metro, Carris trams (including Tram 28), funiculars, and Carris buses within Lisbon municipality. The Navegante monthly pass used by residents remains the best value for stays of a week or more. For stays of 2–4 days, the 24h or 48h visitor passes offer the best flexibility. Buy at any Metro station ticket machine or staffed ticket office. The Zapping reloadable card (€0.50 card fee + credit) is the most practical option for 1–2 day visits.
€6.80 (book) → €7.10 (2026) carris.pt / metrolisboa.pt
CP trains — fare changes
CP — Alfa Pendular and Intercidades fares
CP (Comboios de Portugal) increased Alfa Pendular and Intercidades fares by approximately 4.2% on 1 January 2026. Key routes: Lisbon (Oriente) – Porto (Campanhã) Alfa Pendular now from €16.10 (advance, 2nd class). Lisbon – Faro from €16.80 (advance). Lisbon – Coimbra from €13.20. Book at cp.pt in advance for lowest fares — the cheapest “Alfa” tickets sell out on busy routes. Same-day full-price fares are significantly more expensive. Portuguese train travel remains excellent value compared to other Western European rail networks.
Lisbon–Porto from €15.50 (book) → From €16.10 advance (2026) cp.pt
Major attractions — price changes
Lisbon — Jerónimos Monastery
The Jerónimos Monastery (Mosteiro dos Jerónimos) adult entry is now €12. Online advance booking is now strongly recommended — queues in summer can reach 1.5 hours. Book at patrimoniocultural.gov.pt. The first Sunday of each month, entry remains free — but queues are longest on these days. The adjacent Museu Nacional de Arqueologia is included in a combination ticket. Allow 1.5–2 hours for a full visit including the refectory and cloisters.
€10 (book) → €12, online booking recommended (2026) patrimoniocultural.gov.pt
Lisbon — Torre de Belém
Torre de Belém adult entry increased to €8. The tower is small and visitor numbers are timed — advance booking at patrimoniocultural.gov.pt is now required during April–October to guarantee entry. The terrace at the top offers one of Lisbon’s best Tagus views but the interior is minimal. The first Sunday of each month: free entry, but book in advance even for free Sundays. Padrão dos Descobrimentos (Monument to the Discoveries) across the road: €10, good alternative for Tagus views with fewer queues.
€6 (book) → €8, advance booking required Apr–Oct (2026) patrimoniocultural.gov.pt
Entry requirements
ETIAS — US, UK, Canadian, Australian travelers
ETIAS remains not yet in force for Portugal or any Schengen country as of early 2026, despite repeated delayed launch dates. Check current requirements at etiasvisa.com or the EU official travel portal before departure. When it launches: online, approximately €7, valid three years. Portugal is within the Schengen Area — ETIAS will cover all Schengen countries in one application. UK citizens (post-Brexit) will need ETIAS once active.
Confirmed Correct
Verified March 2026
✓
Lisbon – Porto Alfa Pendular — approximately 2h 45m Confirmed. Multiple daily departures from Lisboa Oriente and Lisboa Santa Apolónia to Porto Campanhã and Porto São Bento. The Oriente departure is generally the most convenient for visitors staying near the waterfront or Marquês de Pombal. Book at cp.pt; advance tickets from €16.
✓
Pastel de Belém (Pastéis de Belém) — original and irreplaceable Confirmed. The original pastéis de nata are still sold exclusively at Pastéis de Belém, Rua de Belém 84–92, using the 1837 recipe that has never left the building. Queue during peak hours, take a number, eat inside. The recipe is a secret known only to a small number of pastry makers. Every “original” sold elsewhere is an imitation — some excellent, none the same.
✓
Lisbon Metro — 4 lines, clean, frequent, best urban transport option Confirmed. The Lisbon Metro runs from approximately 06:30 to 01:00. Clean, air-conditioned, reliable. Covers all major visitor areas: Baixa-Chiado, Rossio, Marquês de Pombal, Oriente, Cais do Sodré. Single ticket €1.61 with Zapping card (reloadable, €0.50 card fee). Do not pay full €2 single journey at the machine when Zapping is available.
✓
Alentejo wine — outstanding quality at honest prices Confirmed. Portugal’s Alentejo region produces some of Europe’s most distinctive wines at prices that remain astonishing by French, Italian, or Spanish comparable standards. Herdade do Esporão, Cartuxa, and Monte da Peceguina are benchmark producers. In Évora, buy from the adega (wine cooperative) rather than tourist shops.
✓
Douro Valley train — one of Europe’s great rail journeys Confirmed. The Linha do Douro from Porto Campanhã to Pinhão (3 hours) follows the Douro river through terraced vineyard landscape for the final hour — the section from Régua to Pinhão is the most dramatic. Take a right-side window seat when departing Porto. Book at cp.pt; sell out quickly in September during harvest.
✓
Porto — port wine lodge tours in Vila Nova de Gaia Confirmed. The south bank of the Douro (Vila Nova de Gaia) is where all the major port lodges are based — Taylor’s, Graham’s, Ramos Pinto, Sandeman, Fonseca. Tours include cellar visit and tasting. Price range €12–25 depending on tasting tier. Book directly with the lodge; walk-up available at most, but booking avoids queues. Graham’s is the most scenic lodge position.
✓
Lisbon free viewpoints (miradouros) — all remain free Confirmed. Miradouro da Graça, Miradouro de Santa Catarina, Miradouro das Portas do Sol, and the Senhora do Monte viewpoint are all free and unrestricted. The Miradouro de Santa Luzia (Alfama) is the most photographed — arrive before 10:00 to avoid tour groups. Miradouro da Graça (slightly further, fewer tourists) has the best panorama of Alfama and the Tejo.
✓
Museu Nacional do Azulejo, Lisbon — €10 Confirmed. The world’s most important azulejo (decorative tile) museum, housed in a 16th-century convent with a baroque church. One of Lisbon’s most undervisited major museums — usually quiet even in peak season. Book at museudoazulejo.pt. Closed Mondays.
✓
Faro — Ria Formosa natural park ferry tours from old town pier Confirmed. Half-day and full-day boat tours of the Ria Formosa lagoon system depart from the pier in Faro’s old town (Portas do Mar). Tours visit barrier islands and birdlife areas. Price range €25–45 depending on duration. Book at tour operators on the pier or in advance online. July–August: Ilha Deserta is accessible by scheduled ferry (€18 return).
New for 2026
Added March 2026
Lisbon — new visitor management measures in historic districts
Lisbon municipality introduced new visitor distribution measures in the Alfama and Mouraria districts in 2025, operationally from 2026. The measures include: restricting group tour sizes in Alfama to a maximum of 25 people; pedestrian flow guidance at Miradouro das Portas do Sol during peak hours; and an informal tour operator registration system for licensed guides in the historic centre. These measures are specifically aimed at reducing the noise and congestion impact on the 3,000+ residents still living in Alfama. For independent travelers, there is no change — the district remains freely accessible. Group tours will feel more regulated.
Porto — new CP urban service to Aveiro integrated
CP’s urban (Urbanos do Porto) service now runs integrated ticketing with the Metro do Porto on a single reloadable Andante card. The Andante card (€0.60) covers Metro, CP Urban trains (including to Braga, Guimarães, and Aveiro), and STCP buses within the Porto metropolitan area on the same journey credit system. For visitors making multiple day trips from Porto, the Andante card is significantly more practical than buying separate CP tickets for each journey. Load credit at Metro stations or CP ticket machines.
Portugal-wide — Airbnb short-term rental restrictions
Portugal’s Mais Habitação (More Housing) legislation introduced in 2023 and now in operational effect has significantly reduced new short-term rental (Alojamento Local) licences in Lisbon and Porto historic districts. The supply of available Airbnb-style apartments in central Lisbon and the Ribeira district of Porto is materially lower than at the time of publication. Budget 2–3 months ahead for accommodation in central Lisbon in peak season, or expect significantly higher prices for good-location properties booked late. The Algarve and secondary cities are less affected.
Algarve — new direct bus services from Faro Airport
EVA Transportes has expanded its direct coach service from Faro Airport to Algarve resort towns in 2026. Direct coaches now run to Albufeira, Lagos, Portimão, and Vilamoura from Faro Airport arrivals, without requiring a change at Faro bus station. Journey times: Faro–Albufeira 45 min, Faro–Lagos 1h 30m. Check current timetables at eva-bus.com — the service runs seasonally with reduced frequency outside peak summer. This significantly reduces transfer hassle compared to the previous taxi/connection bus approach.
Seasonal Alerts
2026 Calendar
!
Sintra — summer weekends, June–September Sintra receives over 4 million visitors a year in a village with a permanent population of around 400. On summer Saturdays, the narrow streets and Pena Palace approach road become genuinely unmanageable between 11:00 and 16:00. The train from Rossio fills to standing capacity. The correct approach: arrive on the first or second train of the day (before 09:00), enter Pena Palace at the 09:30 slot you booked weeks in advance, and leave by 13:00 before the worst crowds build. Mid-week visits in May, June, September, or October are an entirely different experience.
!
Algarve beaches — July and August at absolute capacity Portugal’s Algarve receives over 10 million visitors annually, the large majority in July and August. Popular beaches (Meia Praia, Praia da Marinha, Praia de Odeceixe) reach genuine capacity on peak-summer Saturdays. Accommodation doubles or triples in price. Algarve in May, June, late September, and October is warm (22–28°C), quiet, and significantly cheaper. The book was written for these shoulder months.
!
Festa de Santo António, Lisbon — 12–13 June Lisbon’s biggest popular festival centres on the Alfama and Mouraria districts on the night of 12–13 June. Sardines grilled in the street, music, dancing, and several hundred thousand people in a small area. Hotels book out months ahead. If your dates overlap: embrace the experience but book accommodation 4+ months ahead. Transport is extremely crowded on the night itself.
!
Nazaré big wave surf — October to March The giant waves at Praia do Norte (Nazaré) that reach 20–30 metres are a winter phenomenon. Summer visits to Nazaré show a normal Atlantic beach. If watching big wave surfing is your reason to visit Nazaré, plan for November–January when swells are most reliable. The cliffside viewpoint at Sitio is the correct vantage point. Check current swell forecasts at windguru.cz before travelling specifically for the waves.
✓
Best windows — March to June and September to November Portugal’s spring (March–June) is arguably the most beautiful season: wildflowers in Alentejo and Algarve, full-green Douro terraces, temperatures 18–24°C in Lisbon. Autumn (September–November) brings Douro harvest season, empty Algarve beaches after the crowds leave, and exceptional light quality for photography. These are the seasons the guide was written for.
✓
Douro harvest season — late September to mid-October The Douro Valley port wine harvest (vindima) runs from late September through mid-October depending on the year. Quinta visits during this period are exceptional: the terraces are active, the smells of fermentation fill the valley, and many quintas offer harvest participation experiences. Book Douro accommodation at least 3 months ahead for harvest weeks — the valley’s limited accommodation fills completely.
