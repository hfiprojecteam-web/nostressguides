What Changed
Verified March 2026
Paris transport — multiple changes
Paper tickets being eliminated — action required
Magnetic paper tickets are being phased out permanently. They stopped working on Paris buses in May 2026 and will stop working on the entire metro, RER, and train network in June 2026. If you show up with an old paper ticket at a turnstile after these dates, it will not work. You now need a Navigo Easy card (physical, €2 one-time fee) or the Île-de-France Mobilités app on your smartphone. The book refers to the carnet being phased out — the situation has now gone further: the entire paper ticket system is being retired.
Paris Métro, RER, bus — single ride
Single ticket (Ticket t+) price increased from €2.15 to €2.55 on 1 January 2026. A journey within central Paris (Zones 1–2) covers all métro lines, RER within the city boundary, and bus connections within 90 minutes of validation. The ticket must be loaded onto a Navigo Easy card or smartphone app — no more paper tickets.
€2.15 (book) → €2.55 (2026) RATP official
Paris — Navigo Semaine (weekly pass, all zones)
The Navigo Semaine weekly pass increased to €32.40 for all zones (Zones 1–5). Valid Monday 00:00 through Sunday 23:59 — not seven days from purchase. If you arrive on a Wednesday, the pass runs until Sunday only; you lose Monday and Tuesday. Plan accordingly or use a Navigo Jour daily pass (€8.65) for the partial days. The pass requires a Navigo Découverte card (€5, one-time, bring a small passport photo) or the smartphone app (free, no photo needed). Critical update: CDG Airport no longer sells Navigo Découverte cards as of August 2025. Buy your card at Gare du Nord or any central metro station on arrival.
€30.75 (book) → €32.40 (2026) iledefrance-mobilites.fr
CDG Airport → Paris — RER B
The RER B airport-to-Paris ticket is now a dedicated “Paris Région ↔ Aéroports” ticket at €14 (up from €11.80 listed in the book). The journey to Gare du Nord takes 25 minutes, Châtelet–Les Halles 28 minutes. Trains every 10–15 minutes from approximately 05:00 to 23:30. Load the ticket onto a Navigo Easy card at machines in the terminal. Note: The Navigo Semaine weekly pass (if already loaded) covers CDG on RER B without any additional ticket — the €14 ticket is only needed for single-trip visitors or those without the weekly pass.
€11.80 (book) → €14 (2026) RATP / SNCF
CDG Airport → Paris — RoissyBus
The book mentions the Orlybus reaching Denfert-Rochéreau for €11.50. This refers to Orly airport — that bus service has also changed (see New Tips). More critically: the RoissyBus (direct bus CDG–Opéra) was permanently discontinued on 1 March 2026. It no longer exists. For CDG arrivals, use the RER B (€14, 25–30 min to central Paris) or Metro Line 14 via shuttle. There is no equivalent direct bus replacement.

Attractions
Eiffel Tower — official toureiffel.paris
Prices have increased significantly. The book lists €12–29; the 2026 official price range is €14.80 to €36.70 for adults depending on floor (2nd floor vs. summit) and access method (stairs vs. elevator). The summit by elevator is the most expensive option. Book exactly 60 days ahead when tickets for that date are released — summit slots sell out within hours during peak season. Stairs to the 2nd floor remain available at shorter notice and at lower cost.
€12–29 (book) → €14.80–36.70 (2026) toureiffel.paris
Notre-Dame de Paris
Notre-Dame reopened in December 2024 after five years of restoration following the 2019 fire. Entry to the cathedral itself remains free, but timed tickets are required during busy periods and must be booked in advance at notre-dame.paris or via the official app. The towers and rooftop are a separate ticketed experience and also require advance booking. The exterior and surrounding Île de la Cité are freely accessible at any time. Morning visits before 10:00 are calmest. The book references Notre-Dame in itineraries — all references are now accurate for the reopened building.
Cathedral entry: free (timed ticket required in peak season) notre-dame.paris
Entry requirements
ETIAS — US, UK, Canadian, Australian travelers
The book correctly notes ETIAS has been delayed repeatedly and may be implemented during the lifespan of this guide. As of early 2026, ETIAS is still not in force. Check current requirements at etiasvisa.com or the official EU website before travel. When it does launch: the process will be entirely online, cost around €7, and the authorization will be valid for three years. It is not a visa — it is a pre-authorization similar to the US ESTA. Non-EU citizens who currently need no visa for France will need ETIAS once implemented.

New for 2026
Added March 2026
Paris transport — phone tap-to-ride rolling out
Contactless bank card payment (tap Visa or Mastercard directly at the turnstile) is being gradually rolled out on Paris buses throughout 2026. For the métro, you still need a Navigo Easy card or the smartphone app. If you have NFC on your phone, download the Île-de-France Mobilités app before arrival, create a virtual Navigo Découverte (free, requires a photo), and load your tickets or weekly pass digitally. No physical card, no queue at the machine, no passport photo needed for the phone version.
CDG Airport — Navigo cards not sold at airport
As of August 2025, CDG Airport train stations no longer sell Navigo Découverte cards. If you want the physical card, buy it at Gare du Nord (5-minute walk from the RER B platform) or any major metro station once you arrive in central Paris. The single-journey Paris Région ↔ Aéroports ticket (€14) is still sold at CDG machines and covers the RER B into the city — this is your option for the first leg. Alternatively: set up the digital Navigo on your phone before flying and load the airport ticket digitally.
Notre-Dame — free entry with timed tickets required
The reopened Notre-Dame is drawing enormous crowds. While entry to the cathedral floor remains free, book a timed entry slot in advance at notre-dame.paris during busy periods (spring through autumn, all school holidays). Show up without a booking and you may face a 2–3 hour queue or be turned away if capacity is reached. The towers and roof panorama require a separate paid ticket booked well in advance — these slots release on a rolling basis and sell out quickly in high season.
Orly Airport — Orlybus discontinued, use Metro 14
The Orlybus service between Orly and Denfert-Rochéreau mentioned in the book has been permanently discontinued. The correct route is now Metro Line 14, which reaches Orly Airport directly. The journey to central Paris (Gare de Lyon or Châtelet) takes approximately 30–35 minutes. The Navigo Semaine weekly pass covers this journey within Zone 4. A single trip requires the Paris Région ↔ Aéroports ticket (€14).
TGV — Ouigo and low-cost options
SNCF’s low-cost Ouigo brand operates on many TGV routes at significantly lower prices, sometimes €5–15 for routes that cost €30–50 on standard TGV. The tradeoffs: Ouigo departs from suburban stations (Marne-la-Vallée, Massy, Lyon-Saint-Exupéry) not central Paris stations; no seat selection included; luggage fees apply for anything beyond a small bag; no onboard catering. For short trips or when saving €30+ justifies the suburban station inconvenience, Ouigo represents genuine value. Always compare both options on SNCF Connect before booking.
Seasonal Alerts
2026 Calendar
!
French school holiday zones (Zones A, B, C) — crowding cascade France divides school holidays into three regional zones that stagger their breaks. When Zone C (Paris region) finishes its vacation, Zone A begins. This creates rolling crowd surges at major attractions. The practical effect: anywhere popular fills rapidly during school breaks, which cover roughly two weeks in February, two weeks at Easter, and all of July and August. Check the official calendar (education.gouv.fr) for exact 2026 dates against your travel window.
!
July–August — Paris empties of locals, fills with tourists French residents leave Paris in August. Restaurants, local cafes, and neighborhood shops close for two to four weeks. What remains is tourist infrastructure at peak prices. The Louvre queue is shorter in August than June (fewer school groups), but hotel prices peak, streets swelter at 30–35°C, and the city has lost the Parisian atmosphere you came for. Shoulder season (May–June, September–October) consistently delivers a better experience at 20–40% lower prices.
!
Tour de France — late June / early July 2026 Stage finishes on the Champs-Élysées and stages passing through tourist regions draw massive crowds. Transport disruptions and road closures affect wherever the race passes. The Rivièra coast in particular sees spillover. Plan around it unless watching the race is your purpose.
!
Cannes Film Festival — mid-May 2026 The Rivièra coast, Nice, and Cannes specifically: hotel prices triple, accommodation disappears months ahead. If your dates overlap, book immediately or adjust. The city feels different even if you are not attending.
!
Paris Fashion Weeks — January, March, June, October Twice yearly (womenswear in March and October, menswear/couture in January and June). Hotels in good Paris arrondissements fill and prices spike. If your dates overlap with fashion week and you want a central hotel, book months ahead or accept elevated rates.
✓
Best windows — mid-May to mid-June and September–October Consistent pattern: spring weather, full facilities, prices below summer peak, manageable crowds. The Loire Valley in May. Provence in June before the heat. Normandy in September. Burgundy in October during harvest. These are the months the guide was written for.
✓
Free museum Sundays — November through March, first Sunday of month National museums free for all on these dates. Lines at the Louvre and Orsay start forming before opening. Arrive at opening time or accept a 30–60 minute queue. Worth planning around if your trip falls in this period.
