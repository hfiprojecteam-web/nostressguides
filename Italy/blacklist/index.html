Restaurants to avoid

Updated 2026
Rome

Within 200 metres of the Colosseum

The entire perimeter

No restaurant within a 200-metre radius of the Colosseum represents good value. This is not an exaggeration — it is a documented, consistent pattern. Average pasta dish: €18–26. Average quality: below a supermarket ready meal. The area exists exclusively to extract money from people who haven't eaten since the airport.

Red flags Menu photos on a board outside. Waiter standing in the doorway actively inviting you in. "Tourist menu" sign. Prices not visible before you sit. Any of these: walk past.

Piazza Navona & Campo de' Fiori — all restaurants facing the square

Beautiful square, terrible food

Every restaurant with tables on Piazza Navona or Campo de' Fiori is paying premium rent for the view and recovering it through your bill. A €14 Aperol Spritz here is not a scam — you're paying for the location. What is a scam is ordering food expecting it to be good. Sit for a drink if you want the atmosphere. Eat somewhere else.

The rule In Rome, the more beautiful the piazza, the worse the food. The best meals are always on streets with no view at all.

Vatican area — Via della Conciliazione and surroundings

The Vatican corridor

The street leading to St Peter's Square is one of the most aggressively tourist-oriented dining corridors in Italy. Prices are 40–60% above the Roman average. The restaurants are not bad because they are lazy — they are bad because they don't need to be good. Cross the river to Prati for the same price and actual quality.

Florence

Ponte Vecchio surroundings — 100-metre radius

The bridge tax

Restaurants around Ponte Vecchio charge a location premium with no quality justification. The further you walk from the bridge — especially into Oltrarno on the south side — the better and cheaper the food gets. Ten minutes of walking is worth €8–12 per person.

Where to go instead Walk south over the bridge, turn left, keep walking 8–10 minutes into the residential part of Oltrarno. Those restaurants serve Florentines, not tourists.
Venice

Anywhere within sight of St Mark's Square

The most expensive bad food in Italy

Venice has an undeserved reputation for bad, overpriced food. That reputation is accurate for the tourist corridor from the train station to St Mark's and back. It is entirely inaccurate for Cannaregio, Castello, and the residential parts of Dorsoduro. Two different cities operating under the same name.

Named: avoid Restaurants on the Riva degli Schiavoni waterfront promenade — consistently documented as the worst price-to-quality ratio in Venice. The view costs approximately €12 per plate.
Naples

Piazza del Plebiscito surroundings

Tourist-facing pizzerias near the main square

Naples has the best pizza in the world and the worst tourist-facing pizzerias in Italy, sometimes within 50 metres of each other. Pizzerias with photos of Vesuvius on the menu, English translations before Italian, and a host outside waving at tourists: the pizza will be edible but it will not be what Naples is capable of. The best pizzerias have queues of locals and no street-facing promotion whatsoever.

The Neapolitan rule If they're trying to get you in, don't go in. If there's a queue of Neapolitans outside at 1:00 PM with nowhere to sit, that's the place.

Tours not worth the price

Updated 2026
All cities

All cities

Hop-on hop-off buses

€25–35 per person for recorded commentary on a double-decker. In Rome, traffic means more time stationary than moving. In Florence, the historic centre is largely closed to buses anyway. In Naples, the routes miss most of what matters. Use the money for a 2-hour walking tour with a local guide — you'll learn ten times more.

Rome

Rome

"Vatican by Night" and similar evening packages

Vatican evening openings are genuinely good — less crowded, different light. What is not good value is the "exclusive evening experience" packages at €80–120 per person that bundle a mediocre dinner with the same ticket you could buy independently for €20–25. You're paying €55–100 for a pasta dinner in a rented hall.

Rome

Catacombs "combo" tours sold near the Colosseum

Sellers near the Colosseum offer €45–65 combo tickets for the Colosseum, Roman Forum, and Catacombs in a single day. The Catacombs are 7 kilometres from the Colosseum. This day will be exhausting, rushed, and you will remember nothing. The Catacombs deserve a dedicated half-day. Book them separately and do them on a different day.

Also note Some combo tickets sold on the street include a voucher that still requires queuing at the gate — not actual reserved entry.
Amalfi Coast

Amalfi Coast

Full-day coach tours from Naples or Sorrento

€65–95 per person for 4 hours on a coach, 40 minutes in Positano, 40 minutes in Amalfi, a fixed tourist-menu lunch, and 4 more hours on a coach. The Amalfi Coast requires a private car, the SITA bus (tiered tickets from about €1.80–2.60 per hop, or a €10 day pass covering the whole coast), or a boat to experience properly. Any format that puts 40 people on a schedule destroys the thing you came to see.

Tuscany

Tuscany

Chianti wine tours by minibus from Florence

The concept is sound. The execution at scale has become an assembly line. The wineries on standard routes are not the best in Chianti — they are the ones that can handle 200 tourists a day. If you want a real Chianti experience: rent a car, pick two small estates with appointment-only visits, and stay for lunch. The cost is lower and the memory is real.

Tourist traps & overrated experiences

Updated 2026
Rome

Rome

Around the Trevi Fountain — the perimeter tax

The fountain itself is free and worth visiting. What is a trap: photographers near the fountain who offer to take your photo "for a donation," overpriced Trevi-branded merchandise in surrounding streets, and ice cream shops on the approach at 40–60% above the Rome average. Everything within 50 metres is priced for people who just had an emotional moment and aren't thinking clearly.

Florence

Florence — San Lorenzo market area

Leather market stalls

The outdoor market around San Lorenzo sells leather goods marketed as "genuine Florentine leather" at €20–80. Most is manufactured outside Italy. Genuine Florentine leather is available in Oltrarno from workshops where you can watch the work being done — at similar or slightly higher prices for a product that is actually what it claims to be.

How to spot real Florentine leather The artisan is physically present. There is a workshop behind or beside the shop. No barcode sticker on the back. The vendor does not immediately offer a discount.
Venice

Venice — tourist shops near St Mark's and Rialto

Murano glass sold outside Murano

The Murano glass sold in Venice's tourist shops is almost entirely manufactured in China. The genuine product is made on Murano island by licensed glassblowers and carries the Vetro Artistico® Murano trademark. If the shop is not on Murano and the item does not carry that specific trademark, it is not Murano glass — regardless of what the label says.

All cities

All cities — tourist area shops

Limoncello and olive oil "direct from the producer"

Shops selling limoncello, olive oil, and truffles "direct from the producer" in tourist areas are rarely anything of the kind. The free tasting is a sales technique. The products are the same you'd find in a supermarket, sold at 200–300% markup in a prettier bottle. Excellent olive oil and limoncello are available in any Italian supermarket at honest prices.

Naples & Pompeii

Naples & Amalfi Coast souvenir shops

Cameo jewellery

Cameo carving is a genuine Neapolitan craft tradition. The cameos sold near Pompeii and in Amalfi Coast towns are overwhelmingly mass-produced in resin, not carved from shell. A hand-carved shell cameo costs €80–250, sold by the craftsperson directly or in certified shops. Anything under €30 is resin. Souvenir shops do not distinguish between the two.

Active scams — 2026

Verified Aug 2026
Rome

Termini Station area

Plainclothes police impersonation

Documented and active. Men show unofficial-looking IDs and ask to check your wallet for counterfeit bills. This is not a real police procedure. Say "chiamo la polizia vera" (I'll call the real police) and walk toward the station. Real plainclothes officers in Italy do not perform wallet checks on tourists on the street.

Spanish Steps, Trevi Fountain

Friendship bracelet ambush

Men place a woven bracelet on your wrist before you can react, then demand €10–20 and become confrontational when refused. Keep hands in pockets or crossed in front of you when walking through these areas. If a bracelet is placed on you, remove it immediately and hand it back without engaging further.

Around major sights — Rome, Florence, Venice

Clipboard petition distraction

A person — often appearing to be a child or teenager — asks you to sign a petition for a charitable cause. While you are occupied, an accomplice picks your pocket. Active near the Duomo in Florence, the Colosseum in Rome, and the Rialto in Venice. Do not engage with anyone holding a clipboard near a tourist site. Say "no grazie" and keep walking.

Venice

Marco Polo Airport — water taxi rank

Unlicensed water taxi drivers

Licensed private water taxis from Marco Polo Airport to Venice run about €140–160 for up to 4 people (fixed operator tariff, more with extra luggage or a night surcharge after 20:00–22:00); shared water taxis cost roughly €30–40 per person. Unlicensed drivers inside the arrivals hall lure you in with a lower quote, then add "landing fees," "night surcharges," and "luggage fees" once you are on the water. Use only taxis with the official yellow licence plate on the boat. Book in advance at motoscafivenezia.it or veneziaunica.it.

Naples

Street markets — Porta Nolana, Via Toledo

Electronics and luxury goods at "incredible prices"

Sealed boxes of iPhones, AirPods, sunglasses, and handbags sold at 60–80% below retail. The boxes contain rocks, broken devices, or cheap replicas. This scam has been operating in Neapolitan markets for decades and the technique is unchanged. There is no legitimate reason a sealed iPhone costs €180. If you are shown a box and asked to decide quickly, the answer is always no.

Classic first-timer mistakes

Italy-wide

Trying to do Rome, Florence, Venice, and the Amalfi Coast in 10 days

This itinerary exists everywhere online and it will produce a trip of exhausted commuting with no actual experience of anything. Rome alone requires 4 days to see properly. The Amalfi Coast requires 2–3 days to be worth the journey. Choosing fewer places and staying longer is not a compromise — it is the correct way to travel in Italy.

The rule If your itinerary requires a different city every night, you are not experiencing Italy. You are ticking boxes on a list that will feel hollow within a week of returning home.

Booking accommodation inside the Venice historic centre for a first visit

Accommodation inside Venice's historic islands costs 40–70% more than equivalent quality on the Mestre mainland or in the Lido, a 10–15 minute journey away. For a first visit of 2–3 days, the price premium for waking up "inside Venice" is rarely justified. The exception: if you are staying 4+ nights — the city at 6 AM before the day-trippers arrive is worth paying for.

Renting a car and driving into historic city centres

Every major Italian historic city centre has a ZTL (Zona a Traffico Limitato). Driving in without a permit triggers an automatic fine, typically €80–130 per violation, per camera, though some cities apply higher tiers up to around €335. Many gates offer a reduced fine of about €58 if paid within 5 days. The cameras are not visible. Your rental company adds an administrative fee on top. Never drive into a walled historic centre. Park at the edge, use public transport, or ask your hotel explicitly about permit arrangements.

ZTL signs to recognise Round red circle with "ZTL" in white text, often with active/inactive hours below. If you see this sign, you have already crossed the boundary.

Not knowing the bar vs table price difference

In virtually every Italian bar and café there are two prices: one for drinking at the counter (standing), one for table service. The difference is typically 50–100%. A €1.20 espresso at the bar becomes a €3.50 espresso at a table. Both are legal and posted by law. Neither is a scam. What is a problem is not knowing the difference and feeling overcharged when you were simply making a choice you didn't know you were making.

Buying train tickets on the day of travel

High-speed trains between Italian cities have dynamic pricing identical to airlines. A ticket bought 3 weeks ahead costs €19–29. The same ticket bought at the station the morning of travel costs €59–89. There is no operational difference between the seats. The entire saving is in advance purchase.

Visiting major sights at midday in July and August

The Colosseum at 1:00 PM in August is 38°C with no shade and a 45-minute queue even with a reservation. The same site at 8:30 AM is 24°C, quiet, and dramatically more coherent. Every major outdoor site in Italy should be visited in the first 90 minutes after opening. This is not a preference. It is the difference between an experience and an endurance test.
