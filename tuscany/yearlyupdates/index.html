Getting Around
Florence ZTL fines — increased enforcement
Fines for unauthorized entry into Florence’s ZTL now start at €83 per infraction (up from €80), with repeat violations compounded. The ZTL covers the entire centro storico: active Monday–Friday 07:30–20:00 and Thursday–Saturday until 23:00 in summer. Cameras photograph every plate. If your hotel is inside the ZTL, contact them before arrival to register your plate for a temporary drop-off exemption. Do not trust your GPS — it does not see ZTL boundaries.
Autolinee Toscane — Florence to Siena bus
The Florence–Siena direct bus (75 minutes, €8.50) remains the best option between the two cities — faster and more convenient than the train. Buy tickets at the bus station near Santa Maria Novella or on the Autolinee Toscane app. The service runs every 30–60 minutes on weekdays; Sunday frequency drops significantly. Confirm current schedule at autolineetoscane.it before your trip.
Car rental — insurance and real costs
Base rental rates continue to be deceptive. A week quoted at €180 typically reaches €350–400 after full CDW insurance (€10–20/day), fuel (€80–120), tolls (€30–60), and parking (€15–30/day in cities). Always book through a consolidator such as AutoEurope or Discover Cars. The pressure to buy extra insurance at the counter remains intense — if your travel insurance or credit card covers rental cars, decline it. Avoid the pre-purchased fuel option entirely.

New for 2026
Added March 2026
Uffizi — afternoon discount ticket (from January 2026)
A new discounted ticket for entry after 16:00 costs €20 online or €16 at the ticket office on the day. Crowds thin significantly in the late afternoon. If you are flexible on timing, this is the best-value way into the Uffizi in 2026. Book at tickets.uffizi.it and select the afternoon time slot.
Accademia + Bargello combined ticket (from March 2026)
The Galleria dell’Accademia and Museo Nazionale del Bargello now offer a combined 48-hour ticket for €26. The Bargello holds Donatello’s bronze David, Michelangelo’s early Bacchus, and one of the finest sculpture collections in Italy — with none of the Accademia crowds. An extended 72-hour pass covering all six museums of the new institute costs €38.
Uffizi — nominative tickets since October 2025
All Uffizi tickets are now issued in the purchaser’s name. A government-issued ID must be shown at entry. This applies to both direct bookings and tickets purchased through third-party agents. If buying for a group, all names are required at time of purchase. Resold tickets are no longer valid.
Vasari Corridor, Florence — reopened late 2024
The elevated passageway connecting the Uffizi to Palazzo Pitti, closed for years of restoration, has reopened. Entry requires an Uffizi ticket plus a supplement. Book through tickets.uffizi.it — slots are limited and sell out well ahead. Not essential for a standard Florence visit, but genuinely unique for those with specific interest in the Medici.
EasyPark and MyCicero — parking apps for Tuscany
Both apps now cover most Tuscan cities including Florence, Siena, Lucca, Arezzo, and Pisa. Download before departure — paying via app avoids hunting for ticket machines and lets you extend parking remotely. Florence Fortezza da Basso garage and Parterre lot are the best options for leaving a car outside the ZTL.
Seasonal Alerts
2026 Calendar
Dates that will affect your trip
These dates cause genuine disruption in specific parts of Tuscany. Not all of them are avoidable — but knowing them changes how you plan around them.
!
Easter Week (April 1–6, 2026) Florence sees its single most crowded period of the year. Hotels command peak prices. Uffizi and Accademia require advance booking weeks ahead. Book everything before mid-February.
!
Palio di Siena — July 2 and August 16 Siena is transformed. Hotels triple their rates weeks either side. The Campo fills with 40,000+ people. Standing room only (free), balcony seats €300–500 months ahead. If you want to attend, book accommodation 6+ months ahead. If you want to avoid the chaos, stay out of Siena entirely on those dates.
!
April 25 + May 1 (ponte) Both are Italian national holidays. When they fall close to weekends (as in 2026), they create 4–5 day national holiday bridges. Accommodation prices surge 30% across Tuscany. Domestic tourists flood cities and agriturismo in numbers comparable to August.
!
Ferragosto period (August 10–20) Italian vacation culture at its peak. Local businesses — neighbourhood trattorias, family shops, smaller hotels — close for 1–3 weeks. Tourist infrastructure stays open. Coast and countryside fill with Italian vacationers. Cities become oddly empty of Romans and full of foreigners.
!
Lucca Comics and Games (late October / early November) Italy’s largest comics convention turns Lucca impassable. 300,000+ visitors. Every hotel within 50km books out months ahead. If Lucca is on your itinerary, confirm the exact dates and book accommodation early, or plan to stay elsewhere and day-trip before the convention starts.
✓
Best timing — October 2026 Harvest season in Chianti and Montalcino. Val d’Orcia at its photographic peak. Crowds thin after the first week. Temperatures comfortable. Florence and Siena feel like real cities again. The best overall month for most Tuscany itineraries.
✓
Best low-cost window — January–February Hotel prices 50–60% below peak. Uffizi without a queue. Restaurants cooking for locals not tourists. The trade-off: shorter days, some agriturismo closures, countryside feels dormant. Florence and Siena work well; Chianti and Val d’Orcia less so.
