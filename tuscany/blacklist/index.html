Restaurants to Avoid
7 patterns
Florence
Within sight of the Duomo · Piazza della Signoria · Ponte Vecchio
Any restaurant near Florence’s monuments
The geography of food quality in Florence is entirely predictable. Within sight of the Duomo, Piazza della Signoria, or the Ponte Vecchio, you are paying rent and foot traffic, not cooking. Photo menus, staff soliciting from doorways, pasta worth €8 served at €22. Walk two blocks in any direction and the quality improves while prices drop. The restaurants near the train station and those on the via that connect monuments are similarly unreliable.
Where to go insteadOltrarno (across the Ponte Vecchio, then keep walking). Sant’Ambrogio neighbourhood east of Santa Croce. San Lorenzo market area — not the tourist stalls, the trattorie behind them.
Florence · any restaurant
Hotel breakfast at €15–18
Nearly every hotel in Florence charges €12–18 for a breakfast buffet. A bar breakfast — cornetto and cappuccino, standing at the counter like Florentines do — costs €3–4 and is authentically better. The savings over a week are substantial. The one exception: if the hotel breakfast is included in your rate and the spread is genuinely good, it may be worth staying.
The ruleNever pay extra for hotel breakfast in Tuscany. Walk to the nearest bar and stand at the counter.
Siena
Piazza del Campo perimeter
Restaurants lining the Campo
A plate of pasta on the Campo costs €20–25 for food worth €10. The setting is exceptional — if you want to drink something while sitting in the shell-shaped piazza, order a coffee or a glass of wine and accept the table surcharge knowingly. Do not eat a full meal here. Walk five minutes toward Via di Camollia, the Stalloreggi neighbourhood, or the streets behind the Duomo where trattorias serve the people who actually live here.
San Gimignano · Pisa
Via San Giovanni · Piazza della Cisterna · streets around the tower
Main-street restaurants in high-tourist towns
San Gimignano’s Via San Giovanni, the main street from the gate to the piazzas, is lined with restaurants surviving on tourist turnover. Quality is predictably mediocre; prices are 20–30% above comparable places on side streets. The same pattern holds around the Leaning Tower in Pisa. In both cases: walk off the main axis. In San Gimignano, Via San Matteo heading north and the lanes around Piazza Sant’Agostino have better options. In Pisa, walk ten minutes toward the river.
San Gimignano exceptionGelateria Dondoli in Piazza della Cisterna has multiple world-championship titles and is legitimately the best gelato in Tuscany. The line is worth it.
Florence · Siena · any tourist zone
Menu turistico at a fixed price
A fixed-price tourist menu is a reliable signal that a restaurant has optimised for throughput rather than cooking. €15–20 for water, bread, a forgettable pasta, and a forgettable secondo. The coperto (€2–3) is added on top. If you see “Menu Turistico” in the window, keep walking. The same budget at a market stall, a bakery, or an unremarkable-looking neighbourhood trattoria will produce a better meal.
Lucca · Versilia
Piazza dell’Anfiteatro · Versilia beachfront
Piazza Anfiteatro restaurants and beach club dining
Lucca’s most photogenic square has restaurants that know exactly where they are. The setting is magical; the food is mediocre and the prices inflated. Walk two blocks in any direction. Similarly, beach club restaurants on the Versilia coast serve adequate food at beach premium prices — convenient but rarely memorable. Save serious meals for evenings in town, where restaurants compete for local customers rather than captive tourists.
Val d’Orcia · Chianti
Agriturismo dinners that are not what they seem
A genuine agriturismo dinner — fixed menu of multiple courses using the farm’s own produce, wine included — is one of Tuscany’s great meals. Some agriturismos charge €35–45 per person for this. Others charge the same price for warmed-up frozen pasta and indifferent service. The difference is not visible from a booking website. Check recent reviews specifically mentioning food. Ask when booking: “Is dinner cooked on the farm?” A hesitant answer is your answer.
Tours Not Worth the Money
5 to skip
Florence · city-wide
Third-party resellers for Uffizi and Accademia tickets
The first page of Google results for “Uffizi tickets” is dominated by platforms charging €40–60 for tickets that cost €29 direct. For the Accademia, the same €20 ticket appears at €35–50 on reseller sites. The official websites — tickets.uffizi.it and galleriaaccademiafirenze.it — are straightforward to use and accept international credit cards. The only legitimate reason to use a third party is if you want a guided tour with an actual guide included in the price.
Legitimate alternativeGuided tours that include tickets are reasonable — you pay more but get expertise and context. What is not reasonable is paying €40+ for a ticket that costs €20.
Florence · museum pass
Firenze Card (€85 for 72 hours)
The Firenze Card covers entry to over 70 museums and unlimited city transport for 72 hours. It is only good value if you are visiting five or more paid museums within 72 hours — which almost no leisure traveler does. For a typical Florence visit (Uffizi + Accademia + one other), buying tickets individually costs €50–55. The Card costs €85. Do the math with your actual itinerary before purchasing. The transport element adds marginal value since Florence’s centre is compact and walkable.
Chianti · wine country
Large-group coach wine tours from Florence
Bus tours departing Florence that visit Chianti typically include 40–50 people, spend 20–30 minutes at each winery, and taste the wines that wineries reserve for this kind of volume throughput. The experience has no relationship to what an estate tasting feels like. Costs run €70–120 per person. If you have a car, three targeted winery visits on your own is better in every respect. If you don’t have a car, hire a private driver for a half-day (€200–300 total for two) — you get proper tastings, personal attention, and choose your own estates.
ExceptionSmall-group tours (6–8 people maximum) with a specialist guide are genuinely worthwhile. The large-coach format is what to avoid.
Florence · San Lorenzo
San Lorenzo leather market
The outdoor stalls and many indoor shops around San Lorenzo market sell machine-made goods positioned as Florentine artisan leather. The “made in Italy” claims are often technically accurate but misleading — assembled from imported components in Prato. Quality is inconsistent and the value proposition disappears once you leave the market and compare. Real Florentine leather workshops still exist — they are in quieter streets in the Oltrarno, Santa Croce, and occasionally in Monti-equivalent areas. Look for workshops where someone is actually working, not shops stacked floor to ceiling with identical bags.
Tuscany · countryside
Premium private transfer from Pisa airport to Florence
Private car transfers from Pisa airport to Florence are sold at €80–120 and marketed as convenience. The journey takes 60–75 minutes in a car and 65–80 minutes door-to-door by train (PisaMover to Pisa Centrale + regional train), at a total cost of €13.70 (€5 + €8.70). The comfort differential does not justify a €70–100 premium for most travelers. The legitimate case for a private transfer is large groups with heavy luggage, very late night arrivals, or when heading directly to a countryside destination with no train connection.
Tourist Traps
5 to know
Tuscany · museums and restaurants
Dynamic Currency Conversion on card payments
When paying by card anywhere in Tuscany, you may be asked whether to pay in euros or your home currency. Always choose euros. “Dynamic Currency Conversion” applies a 3–7% markup applied by the terminal, not your bank. This is legal and common at tourist-facing museums, restaurants, and shops. The same applies at ATMs — always choose to be charged in the local currency. Avoid Euronet ATMs (bright yellow/blue machines near tourist sites) entirely — they charge €5–7 per transaction plus poor exchange rates. Use ATMs attached to actual banks.
San Gimignano · Pitigliano · rural areas
Souvenir ceramics and “local artisan” goods at monument prices
Hand-painted ceramics from the most photogenic spots in Tuscany — San Gimignano, Montepulciano, Pienza, Pitigliano — are priced at a 100–200% premium over identical pieces sold one street back or in non-tourist towns. A ceramic bowl at a shop facing the Campo in San Gimignano costs €35; the same piece costs €12 at a market in Arezzo or a pottery workshop in Montelupo Fiorentino. If ceramics are on your shopping list, buy at the source or away from the concentration of cameras.
Florence · any gelateria with artificial colours
Fake gelato — the mounded display
The gelaterias with mountainous, brightly coloured piles displayed in open tubs are almost certainly using artificial colours, flavourings, and frozen air whipped in to increase volume. Real gelato sits in covered metal pots or is stored flat — it does not form three-inch peaks of neon-green pistachio. The price may be similar to quality gelato but the product is not. In Florence, the quality gelaterias are known but not always the most visible. Look for covered display cases, subdued natural colours, and a shorter flavour list. In smaller towns, ask locals rather than following signs.
Quick ruleIf the pistachio is bright green, walk past. Real pistachio gelato is the colour of pistachio: grey-green.
Versilia coast · beach stabilimenti
Beach club costs — the daily rate is not the total
Versilia beach clubs quote daily rates of €30–80 for an umbrella and two chairs. This covers the equipment. Food, drinks, and any services are charged separately at elevated prices — a lunch at a beach club restaurant adds €20–35 per person. Some clubs charge supplements for Wi-Fi, shower facilities, or proximity to the water. Premium front-row positions cost significantly more than back-row prices. Read the full rate card before settling, not after. For most travelers, Lido di Camaiore offers the best balance of quality and transparency in pricing.
Florence · city-wide
Paying for views you can get for free
The Arnolfo Tower at Palazzo Vecchio charges €7–10 for a view. The Campanile next to the Duomo charges €15 (included in the Duomo full pass) for a view. Piazzale Michelangelo is free and offers the most photographed Florence panorama. The Pincio-equivalent in Florence is the Rose Garden (Giardino delle Rose) on the hill above the Arno — free, less crowded, equally dramatic at golden hour. The rooftop of La Rinascente department store near Piazza della Repubblica is free to access and offers a direct eye-level view of the Duomo.
Active Scams 2026
5 active
Florence · Santa Maria Novella station · buses to Piazzale Michelangelo
Pickpockets — concentrated in specific zones
Tuscany’s pickpocket risk is almost entirely limited to Florence, and within Florence to specific zones: Santa Maria Novella station, the crush around the Duomo, Ponte Vecchio at midday, and the buses toward Piazzale Michelangelo. Teams work in pairs — one causes a distraction or creates crowding, the other takes the wallet. Phones in back pockets, bags worn on the back, and loose pockets are primary targets. The rest of Tuscany — Siena, Lucca, the countryside, small towns — presents negligible pickpocket risk by comparison.
ProtectionFront crossbody bag in Florence. Phone in front pocket. Hand on bag in any crush. Nothing of value in back pockets.
Tuscany · car travel
ZTL fines — automatic, no sympathy, sent months later
ZTL cameras photograph every unauthorised plate. Fines arrive at your home address from 3 to 12 months after your trip, with the rental car company adding a handling fee of €30–50 on top of the €83+ infraction. Florence is the most dangerous: the ZTL covers the entire historic centre, active daily from 07:30. Siena is active 24 hours in the core. Pienza, Lucca, and many hill towns have daytime restrictions. GPS navigation does not see ZTL boundaries and will route you through them. If your hotel is inside a ZTL, call before arrival so they can register your plate for a temporary drop-off exemption.
Most common versionTraveler follows GPS into Florence to reach the hotel, receives three separate €83 fines (entering, exiting, and re-entering), plus €150 in handling fees from the rental company. Total: €400 in unexpected charges.
Winery roads · Chianti · Val d’Orcia
Wine tasting bait and switch
Some wineries advertise free tastings online or with roadside signs. On arrival, the tasting turns out to be free only with a minimum purchase, or the “free” wine is the bulk entry-level product while the experience described online was for a paid premium tasting. Not universal — many wineries genuinely offer free basic tastings — but common enough to confirm terms before arriving. Call or email the day before: ask exactly what the tasting includes and whether there is a cost or purchase requirement.
Florence · Siena · Pisa
Restaurant overcharging — coperto misuse
Rare but real. The coperto (cover charge, €2–4 per person) is legal and standard at every sit-down restaurant. What is not legal: charging coperto twice, adding a “service charge” on top of it, or listing prices on a menu outside that differ from the bill. Check that the menu prices match what was charged. If the bill includes an unexplained line item beyond coperto, ask for itemisation — politely, calmly. The legitimate coperto is non-negotiable; anything else can be disputed.
PreventionTake a photo of the menu before ordering. This makes disputes simple to resolve.
Florence · tourist areas
Friendship bracelet and rose sellers
Men approach and press a bracelet onto your wrist or flowers into your companion’s hands as a “gift,” then demand payment aggressively once accepted. The same pattern operates in Florence as in Rome. Say “No grazie” firmly while still walking — do not slow down, do not touch the bracelet or the flowers. Once a bracelet is on your wrist, extraction is unpleasant. This is concentrated in the Duomo area, Piazza della Repubblica, and around Ponte Vecchio.
Classic First-Timer Mistakes
8 mistakes
Planning
Renting a car for the entire trip including Florence
A car parked in Florence for three days costs €75–105 in parking fees alone, plus the ZTL risk. Florence works on foot and by tram. The optimal strategy for most travelers: arrive by train, spend Florence days without a car, pick up the rental when leaving for the countryside, return it at the end of countryside days before any further city stops. Renting for the full trip adds €200–300 in unnecessary costs.
Best pickup pointFortezza da Basso area, just outside the ZTL. Walking distance from the centre. Returns your parking costs to zero while in Florence.
Florence
Not booking Uffizi and Accademia in advance
April through September, arriving at either museum without a ticket means joining a queue that can exceed 90 minutes — often to be told the day is sold out. The Uffizi afternoon discount ticket (entry after 16:00) is easier to secure and costs less. The Accademia is slightly less difficult but still requires planning. Book 2–4 weeks ahead during high season. Outside peak months, a week ahead usually works. Same-day tickets are possible in winter but cannot be relied upon.
San Gimignano
Arriving between 10:00 and 17:00 in summer
San Gimignano receives day-trip buses from Florence, Siena, and the coast. They arrive from 10:00 and depart by 17:00. Between those hours in summer, the town’s main street is barely navigable. The same towers, the same frescoes, the same gelato — in a completely different atmosphere — are available before 10:00 or after 17:00. If you can stay overnight, the transformation after the day-trippers leave is dramatic. If you cannot, arrive as early as possible and leave before noon.
Countryside travel
Expecting bus service to replace a car in rural Tuscany
The bus network exists on paper for most Tuscan countryside destinations. In practice, routes to Chianti villages, Val d’Orcia, Maremma, and Garfagnana run 3–5 times daily on weekdays and skeletal or not at all on Sundays. Schedules are designed for commuters, not tourists. Depending on buses for vineyard visits, countryside exploration, or small village access will cause significant frustration. Accept that rural Tuscany requires a car and plan accordingly. The Florence–Siena bus and Florence–Lucca–Pisa trains work well; everything beyond the main rail cities does not.
Agriturismo booking
Booking in high season without reading the cancellation policy
Many agriturismos in Chianti and Val d’Orcia require weekly minimum stays in July and August, have strict no-refund policies, and charge full price for no-shows. Cancellation fees of 50–100% of the booking are common during peak season. Travel insurance is essential for bookings of this kind. Read the policy at booking, not when plans change. Properties that fill in April for September are not being aggressive — this is how the category works.
Driving
Ignoring ZTL signs because GPS said to turn
Google Maps and most GPS navigation systems do not account for ZTL zones. They will route you through restricted areas without any warning. The cameras are not visible, the fines arrive months later, and neither the camera system nor the rental company offers exceptions for “the GPS told me to.” Before driving into any historic town centre in Tuscany, check whether it has a ZTL. Park outside, walk in. This applies to Florence, Siena, Lucca, Pienza, Pisa, and dozens of smaller towns.
Dining
Eating dinner at 19:00 or earlier
Tuscan restaurants typically open for dinner at 19:30 at the earliest, more commonly 20:00. Arriving at 19:00 means sitting in an empty restaurant that has not yet warmed up its kitchen or its atmosphere, and often receiving food from a team that is still setting up. The experience improves considerably from 20:00 onward. Families travelling with young children can usually negotiate an earlier seating at more flexible trattorias, but 18:30 is simply not how Italian restaurants operate.
Florence
Basing in Florence and day-tripping everywhere
Florence is the correct base for a first visit. For a longer trip, basing everywhere in Florence and day-tripping to Siena, Lucca, and the countryside means spending significant time on buses and trains instead of in places. Siena deserves two days of its own, not a rushed day trip. Chianti cannot be done properly as a day trip without a car. The Val d’Orcia is 90 minutes from Florence by the most direct route — a day trip is a commute. Move your accommodation as you move through the region. The money saved on transport makes the accommodation change worthwhile.
