Venice Access Fee — The Biggest Change
Day visitors · Historic centre only · 08:30–16:00
The Venice Access Fee (Contributo di Accesso) introduced in 2024 continues in 2026 with a two-tier pricing system not reflected in the book. The fee is €5 if booked at least 4 days in advance and €10 if booked within 3 days of your visit. The book states a flat €5 — book early to get that price.

Who pays: day visitors only (not staying overnight in Venice). Who is exempt: overnight guests, children under 14, Veneto residents, students enrolled in Venice.

Critical note for overnight guests: Even if you are staying in a hotel and are fully exempt from payment, you must still register online and obtain a QR code to show during inspections. Your hotel may handle this automatically — confirm before arrival. Register at cda.ve.it. Fines for non-compliance run €50–300.

Vaporetto & Transport
ACTV — single ride
Single ride confirmed at €9.50. This remains one of the most expensive single public-transport tickets in Europe — a 10-minute trip costs the same as a 40-minute one. Any visit involving more than two rides in a day makes a pass better value. Buy passes at ACTV booths (Piazzale Roma, Ferrovia, Rialto, San Marco) or via the AVM Venezia Official App. Always validate before boarding — inspectors check regularly; fines are €60 plus the fare.
€9.50 single — confirmed actv.avmspa.it
ACTV — tourist time passes
All pass prices confirmed unchanged for 2026. The clock starts at first validation, not at purchase. Buy in advance but validate only when you are ready to travel. Passes cover vaporetti, mainland buses (Mestre), and the People Mover. Not valid on Alilaguna (airport service), lines 16 and 19, or water taxis.

Museums & Attractions
Palazzo Ducale (Doge's Palace)
Price confirmed at €30 including Museo Correr, Museo Archeologico, and Biblioteca Marciana. Valid for 3 months. In high season, book online to secure timed entry and reduce indoor queuing. The Itinerari Segreti (Secret Itineraries guided tour) remains at €28 supplement and must be booked well in advance through the Musei Civici website — it accesses normally closed areas including Casanova's cell and the torture chambers.
€30 — confirmed palazzoducale.visitmuve.it
Basilica di San Marco — skip-queue booking
Entry to the basilica remains free but the online skip-queue reservation is now €3 per person (up from previous years when it was free or lower). In high season the standard queue runs 30–60 minutes. Book at venetoinside.com. Shoulders and knees must be covered — enforced at the door. Bags larger than a small purse must be checked at the free deposit nearby (Ateneo San Basso, Piazzetta dei Leoncini). No photography inside.
Reservation: €3 (entry still free) venetoinside.com
Alilaguna water bus — airport to Venice
Price confirmed at €15 one-way, €27 return. Boats every 30 minutes from approximately 06:00 to midnight. The Orange Line reaches San Marco in 75 minutes; the Blue Line via Lido in 90 minutes. Note: Alilaguna is not covered by ACTV passes. The ATVO bus to Piazzale Roma remains a cheaper alternative at €14 one-way, €24 return and takes about 25 minutes to Piazzale Roma.
€15 one-way / €27 return — confirmed alilaguna.it

Added March 2026
Access Fee — overnight guests still need a QR code
If you are staying overnight in Venice, you are exempt from paying the Access Fee — but you are not exempt from registering. On applicable dates, you must obtain an exemption QR code at cda.ve.it or through your hotel. Many hotels now handle this automatically and email the code with your booking confirmation. If yours does not, register yourself. Inspectors check documents including at the train station and Piazzale Roma. Showing nothing when asked is treated the same as not paying.
Islands not subject to Access Fee
In 2026, the Access Fee does not apply to visits to the minor lagoon islands: Murano, Burano, Torcello, Lido, Pellestrina, and smaller islands. If you enter Venice only to take a vaporetto to Murano without setting foot in the historic centre, no fee is required. However, stopping in the historic centre en route on a chargeable date triggers the obligation.
Visiting after 16:00 — fee does not apply
The Access Fee applies only between 08:30 and 16:00 on chargeable dates. Arriving in Venice after 16:00 for dinner, a concert, or an evening walk does not require payment or registration, even for day visitors. This is a practical option for those staying on the mainland (Mestre, Padova) who want to experience Venice in the evening.
San Marco Basilica — bag deposit before entry
Bags larger than a small purse or daypack are not permitted inside and must be checked before entry at the Ateneo San Basso on Piazzetta dei Leoncini, about 1 minute from the main entrance. The service is free. Factor this into your timing: the deposit has its own queue in peak season. Backpacks, rolling luggage, and large shoulder bags are all affected.
AVM Venezia Official App — buy passes on your phone
The official ACTV app now allows purchasing and validating vaporetto passes directly on your phone without needing a physical card or queuing at booths. Download AVM Venezia Official before arrival. The app also shows real-time vaporetto arrivals at every stop — useful for avoiding long waits when a different line or walking would be faster.
Seasonal Alerts
2026 Calendar
Dates that will affect your visit
Venice is affected by its own events, Italian national holidays, and the Access Fee calendar. These are the dates that require specific planning.
!
Carnevale (late February — dates vary with Easter) Crowds rival August, prices match. Piazza San Marco becomes impassable during peak Carnevale afternoons. If you want Carnevale, book accommodation 3–4 months ahead; prices triple. If you do not want Carnevale, avoid the two weekends and the week between.
!
Easter weekend (April 4–6, 2026) Italian families flood the city for the long weekend. Restaurants require reservations days ahead. Piazza San Marco impassable. All three days fall within the Access Fee window. Book accommodation by January.
!
April 25 + May 1 ponte (Apr 24–30 / Apr 29–May 3) Both national holidays fall within a major Access Fee window (April 24–30 is entirely subject to the fee). The bridge weekends Italians construct around these dates push accommodation to peak prices across the whole region.
!
Festa del Redentore (third weekend of July — July 18–19, 2026) Saturday night fireworks over the Giudecca Canal are extraordinary. Boats crowd every waterway. The city fills with Venetians reclaiming their city for one night. The Access Fee applies. If you want the fireworks, arrive before 22:00 and stay on the Zattere or Giudecca waterfront.
!
Acqua alta season (October–March) With MOSE now operational, the most extreme events (>130 cm) are largely prevented. Moderate events (100–110 cm) still flood Piazza San Marco and low quaysides for 2–3 hours. Download the Ci.Venezia app. Rubber overshoes (€5–10) available everywhere in the city. Follow the raised passerelle walkways.
!
New Year's Eve — Piazza San Marco concert A public concert and midnight fireworks. The piazza fills from early evening. Prices spike from Christmas through January 6 (Epiphany) and then drop sharply. January 2–early February is among the cheapest and quietest periods of the year.
✓
Best timing for most visitors — late May, early June Spring warmth, long days, Access Fee applies only on weekends. Crowds below summer peak. The city is fully operational. Outdoor dining works. Islands are rewarding.
✓
Best low-cost window — November (excluding Salute weekend) to mid-December Minimum tourists, lowest prices, atmospheric fog. Acqua alta possible but manageable with MOSE. The Accademia without a queue. Bacari without a wait. The city feels inhabited again.
