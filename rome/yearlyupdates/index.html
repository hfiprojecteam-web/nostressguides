What Changed
Verified March 2026
Vatican
Vatican Museums + Sistine Chapel
Standard adult ticket raised. The online booking fee also increased from €4 to €5. Always book on the official site — museivaticani.va — to pay the correct price. Third-party resellers charging €40–60 are selling the same ticket at a markup.
€17 + €4 booking → €20 + €5 booking = €25 total museivaticani.va
Sistine Chapel — Last Judgment
Michelangelo's Last Judgment underwent extraordinary restoration work January–March 2026. The chapel remained open throughout but the altar wall was partially obscured by scaffolding during the restoration period. Work completed March 2026 — the full fresco is again fully visible.
Major Sites
Colosseum + Roman Forum + Palatine Hill
Standard ticket price confirmed at €18. Tickets are now nominative — your name is on the ticket and ID is checked at the entrance. Book at ticketing.colosseo.it (opens 30 days before your visit). Third-party resellers sell the same ticket at €40–60+ — never use them. The Colosseum Underground, Arena Floor, and Attic are separate upgraded tickets.
€18 confirmed ticketing.colosseo.it
Colosseum — booking now opens 30 days ahead only
A 2025 regulation change means tickets go on sale exactly 30 days before your visit date — not months ahead. Underground and Attic tickets still sell out within minutes of release. Standard tickets are now easier to get than in previous years.
Transport
ATAC — Tap & Go best fare
Paper tickets remain at the same prices. However, if you use contactless payment (card or phone) and make 6 or more journeys in 24 hours, the system automatically caps your charge at €8.50 — not the paper-pass rate of €7. Both are valid options; the contactless cap is slightly higher but requires no separate purchase.
Trams 3, 8, 14 — temporary service changes
Rome's tramway modernisation project is affecting lines 3, 8, and 14 throughout 2026. Check ATAC's website or the Moovit app for current disruptions before using these lines. Bus replacements operate on the same routes during works.
Jubilee 2026 Specific
Vatican area — extended closures and crowd events
The Holy Year brings 30–35 million pilgrims to Rome. St. Peter's Square closes for major Catholic feast days and papal events — sometimes with no advance notice. Check the Vatican's official calendar before planning a Vatican day. Accommodation near Prati, Borgo, and Via della Conciliazione is running 15–25% above normal rates.
Pilgrim routes — pedestrian restrictions
Designated pilgrimage routes run through parts of Centro Storico and toward the four major basilicas. On high-traffic days (Easter week, Corpus Christi, Assumption, All Saints) some streets become one-way or restricted. Plan extra time for any cross-city walk during these dates.
Basilica di San Paolo fuori le Mura — increased visitors
San Paolo has one of Rome's four Holy Doors, which draws significantly more pilgrims than usual during the Jubilee year. The basilica remains free and uncrowded compared to St. Peter's — but busier than before. Visit before 10:00 for calm.

New for 2026
Added March 2026
Colosseum — MyColosseum official app
The Colosseum's official free app (iOS and Android) now includes a free interactive audio guide in Italian and English that follows you automatically as you move through the monument. Replaces the €7 rental guide. Download before arriving — Wi-Fi is unreliable inside.
Contactless payment — now accepted on most ATAC vehicles
Tap-and-go contactless payment (card, Apple Pay, Google Pay) now works on Metro turnstiles and increasingly on buses and trams. Still validate your payment at readers — travelling without validation means a fine even with a valid contactless card. The Moovit app remains more accurate than Google Maps for real-time bus information.
Vatican Museums — new ticketing rules
Tickets are now issued in the purchaser's name (like the Colosseum). A government-issued ID must be shown at entry. This is designed to prevent reselling — but means buying for multiple people requires all their names at the time of purchase.
Friday night openings — Vatican Museums
Friday evening openings (April–October, typically 19:00–23:00) continue in 2026. Tickets €21. Smaller crowds, better light, different atmosphere. One of the best ways to experience the Sistine Chapel without the daytime crush. Book well in advance — slots sell out.
Domus Aurea — weekends only, advance booking
Nero's Golden House continues weekend visits with virtual reality experiences. €16–20, guided 75–90 min tours. Closed first Sunday of the month. Entrance from Via della Domus Aurea — different from the Colosseum entrance. Book via CoopCulture.
Colosseum Attic level (3rd–5th tier) — limited availability
The attic levels with panoramic views are among the hardest Colosseum tickets to get — sometimes harder than the Underground. Released 30 days ahead, sold out within minutes. If this is a priority, set a reminder and check ticketing.colosseo.it at the exact time the slots open.
Jubilee 2026 — Practical Impact
Holy Year
What is the Jubilee?
2026 is a Catholic Holy Year. The Vatican estimates 30–35 million pilgrims will visit Rome — roughly double the normal level of religious tourism. This affects primarily the Vatican area and the four major basilicas (St. Peter's, San Giovanni in Laterano, San Paolo fuori le Mura, Santa Maria Maggiore).
!
Vatican Museums — book 3–4 weeks ahead minimum High-demand Jubilee periods (Easter week, feast days) may require even more lead time. Friday night openings sell out earliest.
!
Dates to avoid if possible Easter Week (April 1–6, 2026) is the single most congested period. April 25 + May 1 bridge creates domestic tourist surge. Major feast days: Corpus Christi (June), Assumption (August 15), All Saints (November 1).
!
Accommodation near Vatican — 15–25% premium Properties in Prati, Borgo, and along Via della Conciliazione command elevated rates all year. Staying in Trastevere, Monti, or Testaccio avoids the daily pilgrim traffic and saves money.
✓
Colosseum, Borghese, and neighbourhood Rome — unaffected The Jubilee primarily affects religious sites and the Vatican area. The Colosseum, Galleria Borghese, Testaccio, Monti, and other neighbourhood life continue normally.
✓
Non-religious visitors still have an excellent trip Visit St. Peter's at opening (07:00) or late afternoon. Use Option C from the guide (two half-days) for the Vatican — it remains the most effective approach in Jubilee conditions.
