Active scams & warnings

Verified Mar 2026

Rome

Termini Station area — Plainclothes police impersonation

Men in civilian clothing approach tourists, show unofficial-looking badges, and ask to inspect your wallet for "counterfeit euros." This is not a real police procedure. Italian law enforcement never performs wallet checks on the street. Walk away calmly toward the station entrance. If pressured, say "chiamo la polizia" (I'll call the police) and enter the station.

Colosseum exterior — Fake "skip the line" sellers

Individuals near the Colosseum sell "official skip-the-line tickets" for €35–60 in cash. No authorised resellers exist outside the official site. The Colosseum has no physical ticket office that sells same-day unreserved access — everything is online or through licensed tour operators only.

Spanish Steps / Trevi Fountain — Friendship bracelets

Men place a woven bracelet on your wrist without asking, then demand €10–20. Once it's on your wrist the confrontation escalates. Keep hands in pockets near these sites, or decline firmly before contact is made.

Florence

Around Piazza del Duomo — Fake petition / clipboard distraction

Groups, often including children, ask you to sign a petition. While distracted, an accomplice pickpockets bags or jacket pockets. Do not engage. This scam has been active near the Duomo and Ponte Vecchio since 2023 and remains common as of 2026.

Venice

Gondola pricing — Non-posted rates

The official fixed gondola rate is €90 daytime (9:00–19:00) / €120 evening for a maximum of 6 people, 30-minute ride. Any gondolier quoting more, or proposing a "private special" in a side canal, is charging above the official tariff. The rate is municipal — it is non-negotiable and legally fixed.

Naples

Centrale Station surroundings — Unsolicited car "minders"

Men approach parked cars and offer to "watch" the vehicle in exchange for cash. This is organised extortion. Use only enclosed car parks with automatic barriers and a staffed cashier. The guide's recommended lot remains legitimate as of this update.

All destinations

Restaurant menus — Cover charge ("pane e coperto")

Legal and standard across Italy: €1.50–4 per person, added automatically to the bill. Not a scam. What is a scam: restaurants near major sights that don't display a menu with prices outside. The law requires visible pricing. Walk past any restaurant that won't show you the menu before you sit.

New since publication

Added Mar 2026

Rome

Trastevere — Gelateria update

Fior di Luna (Via della Lungaretta 96) has changed ownership. Multiple reports of declining quality. The alternative cited in the guide — Fatamorgana (Prati branch, Via Trionfale) — remains the best natural-ingredient gelato in Rome.

Testaccio — Worth adding to your map

The Testaccio covered market (Via Galvani) has become significantly more food-forward since 2024. Stalls 15 and 23 serve some of the best supplì and trippa in Rome. Open Monday–Saturday, 7:00 AM–3:00 PM. Cash only for most stalls.

Naples

Quartieri Spagnoli — New find

Trattoria da Emilia (Via Speranzella 33) — opened 2025. No tourist menu. Lunch €12–15, cover included. A white-tablecloth trattoria run by a Neapolitan family for 30 years in the same building, recently reopened under the original name. Closed Sundays.

Venice

Cannaregio — Overlooked neighbourhood alert

Cannaregio has overtaken Dorsoduro as the most reliable area for non-tourist-trap dining in Venice. The guide's Dorsoduro recommendations remain valid, but if you're staying beyond 2 days, add a dinner walk through Cannaregio — the area around Rio della Sensa is particularly good.
