Verified August 2026
Deutsche Bahn — fares and passes
Deutsche Bahn — long-distance fares frozen, but book early anyway
Deutsche Bahn kept its long-distance base fares — Flexpreis, Sparpreis, and Bahncard prices — unchanged at the winter 2025/26 timetable change. It is the first year without a fare increase in six years, and it applies to both flexible and Spar/Super-Sparpreis tariffs. Super Sparpreis (advance purchase) ICE tickets still start from €17.90 nationwide, bookable via the DB Navigator app or bahn.de. That said, a July 2026 consumer-association (vzbv) investigation found the cheapest advertised Sparpreis fares were frequently unavailable in practice — real booked prices ran 15–157% above the advertised minimum on the majority of days checked, with the widest gaps on popular routes. Treat “from €17.90” as a floor, not a guarantee: book as early as possible and compare several dates in the app’s calendar view. Flex tickets (anytime travel) remain far more expensive than Sparpreis on the ICE network.
Fares frozen for winter 2025/26 (no increase) → Super Sparpreis still from €17.90 bahn.de / vzbv
Germany-wide — Deutschlandticket
The Deutschlandticket (monthly subscription covering all regional trains, S-Bahn, U-Bahn, trams, and buses in Germany, excluding ICE/IC) increased to €63 per month on 1 January 2026 — the second consecutive January increase, up from €58 in 2025 and €49 at its 2023 launch. It remains extraordinary value for visitors spending a week or more in Germany making regional journeys. Key limitation: the Deutschlandticket does NOT cover ICE, IC, or EC (Eurocity) express trains — only regional (RE/RB) and urban (S-Bahn/U-Bahn) services. The Bayern-Ticket (€34 for the first person, plus €8 per additional traveller up to 5, total €66) remains valid as a regional Bavaria-only day pass and is often cheaper for short Bavaria-only trips.
€58/month (2025) → €63/month (2026) bahn.de
Neuschwanstein — booking changes
Neuschwanstein: no walk-up tickets available — advance booking mandatory
Since 2023, Neuschwanstein Castle has operated a fully mandatory advance booking system with no walk-up ticket sales at the castle or the Ticket Centre in Hohenschwangau village. Book exclusively at neuschwanstein.de. Adult interior tour: €21, plus a €2.50 processing fee per ticket for online booking. Hohenschwangau Castle (the yellow castle below Neuschwanstein) is now €19.50. Tickets open around 8 weeks ahead and peak-season slots (July–August, weekends in May–June) sell out within hours of release. The closest available slot for the interior tour is often 2–3 weeks out in summer. The exterior grounds and Marienbrücke viewpoint remain freely accessible without a ticket — the classic castle photograph does not require an entry ticket.
Major museum and cathedral price changes
Cologne — Cologne Cathedral (Dom) is no longer free for tourists
Starting 1 July 2026, the Cologne Cathedral chapter introduced a mandatory €12 “Besichtigungsgebühr” (viewing fee) for tourist visits — ending the cathedral’s longstanding free entry. The €12 ticket now covers the interior, the treasury (Schatzkammer), and the south tower climb (Turmbesteigung) together, which previously cost roughly €6 each separately. A reduced €6 rate applies to students 14+, trainees, and NRW Sozialpass holders; children up to 13 remain free for all three. Worship, private prayer, and candle-lighting remain free via the north portal (Hauptbahnhof side) — only sightseeing visits require a ticket. Free days: 1 May, 3 October, and the annual “Nacht der offenen Kirchen” in March. Tickets can be booked up to 3 months ahead (max 9 per booking) at koelner-dom.de/tickets, or at the new ticket counter in the Kurienhaus on Roncalliplatz.
Free entry, tower €6 (old model) → €12 combined ticket (from 1 Jul 2026) koelner-dom.de
Berlin — Museumsinsel (Museum Island) tickets restructured
The Staatliche Museen zu Berlin now runs a clearer two-tier ticket structure. The Museumsinsel-Ticket (day ticket, €24) covers all Museum Island venues — Bode Museum, Alte Nationalgalerie, Altes Museum, Neues Museum, Friedrichswerdersche Kirche, and the temporary exhibition “Pergamonmuseum. Das Panorama”. For broader access, the new Museum Pass Berlin (3-day ticket, €32) covers 30+ museums across the whole city, including all Museum Island venues. The main Pergamonmuseum itself — including the Pergamon Altar — remains closed for renovation, with an officially confirmed reopening of 4 July 2027 (previously only “2027 or later”). Individual museum entries run €10–12 each. Advance booking at smb.museum is recommended for peak season weekends.
3-day pass concept (unclear) → Day ticket €24 · citywide 3-day pass €32 smb.museum
Munich — Deutsches Museum
The Deutsches Museum adult entry rose by €1 on 28 January 2026 and is now €16 (reduced €9; family ticket €33). The museum has completed the first phase of its major renovation (the longest in its 120-year history) — several wings that were closed are now reopening through 2026. The full renovation is not expected to complete until 2028. Check deutsches-museum.de for the current open section map before planning your visit — the closing and reopening sequence of wings is ongoing. The museum still justifies a full day with what is currently accessible. Closed on major Bavarian public holidays.
€15 (previous) → €16, partial renovation ongoing (2026) deutsches-museum.de
Berlin — Reichstag dome registration window corrected
The Reichstag rooftop dome remains free to visit, registered at visite.bundestag.de with name and date of birth. Slots typically open around 8 weeks (2 months) ahead of the visit date — not the fixed 90-day window previously listed here. During peak season (June–September and school holidays) the most popular slots (10:00–13:00 on weekends) fill within about 2 weeks of release, so register the moment your dates are confirmed. A short-notice option also exists: the on-site Service-Außenstelle in front of the building can book leftover slots from 2 hours up to 2 days ahead, subject to availability — useful if you didn’t plan ahead. Do not book via any third-party site — the only legitimate, free booking channel is bundestag.de / visite.bundestag.de.
Entry requirements
ETIAS — US, UK, Canadian, Australian travelers
ETIAS remains not yet in force for Germany or any Schengen country as of August 2026. In mid-July 2026 the EU removed its previously stated “last quarter of 2026” launch target from the official ETIAS website, after internal assessments (linked to delays in the separate Entry/Exit System, EES) concluded a 2026 launch was no longer feasible. A 2027 launch is now considered more likely, with a revised timeline expected after the eu-LISA agency’s September 2026 board meeting. Check current requirements at etias.com or the EU official portal before departure. When active: online application, a fee of approximately €20 (free for under-18s), valid 3 years. Germany is fully within Schengen — one ETIAS will cover all Schengen countries. UK travelers (post-Brexit) will need ETIAS once launched.

Bayern-Ticket — €34 for 1 person, all regional trains in Bavaria all day Confirmed, price updated. The Bayern-Ticket covers all RE, RB, S-Bahn, and most bus services in Bavaria from 09:00 Mon–Fri (all day Sat–Sun) until 03:00 the following morning. Additional travelers: €8 each (max 5 people, total €66). Valid for day trips from Munich to Füssen (Neuschwanstein), Salzburg, Nuremberg, Rothenburg, and beyond. Buy at any DB station machine or the DB Navigator app.
✓
Würzburg Residence — €10, Tiepolo ceiling included Confirmed, price updated (regular €10, reduced €9). The staircase ceiling fresco by Giovanni Battista Tiepolo (1752–53, 677 sqm) remains the largest fresco in the world and is included in the standard entry ticket. Entry to the Hofkirche and Hofgarten is free. Book at residenz-wuerzburg.de. Tours in English at fixed times. Closed Mondays in winter (November–March).
✓
Berlin — East Side Gallery free Confirmed. The longest remaining section of the Berlin Wall (1.3 km, East Side Gallery) remains freely accessible 24 hours a day along Mühlenstraße. No ticket, no booking, no time restriction. The murals were last restored in 2009. Outdoor mural art of this scale and historical weight, freely accessible in the middle of a major city, is genuinely rare in the world.
✓
Elbphilharmonie Hamburg — plaza free, concerts require tickets Confirmed. The Elbphilharmonie’s public plaza (8th floor, via the curved escalator) remains free to access with a time-slot ticket (free, booked at elbphilharmonie.de). Concerts in the main hall sell out months ahead and start from €10 for restricted-view seats. Even without a concert ticket, the architecture and harbour view from the plaza are among the best free experiences in Germany.
✓
Nuremberg Christmas Market — 1–24 December, free entry Confirmed. The Christkindlesmarkt on Nuremberg’s Hauptmarkt is the most historically rooted Christmas market in Germany (since 1628) and remains free to enter. Operating hours: Mon–Sat 10:00–21:00, Sun 10:00–20:00. The market is at capacity on weekends — weekday mornings before 13:00 are significantly more atmospheric. The Frauenkirche Glockenspiel (daily at noon) is free to watch.
✓
Rhine Valley cruise — KD Line, Loreley section approximately €18–24 one way Confirmed. The Loreley section (Rüdesheim to St Goarshausen, or Bacharach to Boppard) is the most dramatic part of the Rhine Gorge. Southbound (upstream) is slower but the view of castles on both banks is sustained. Buy at KD kiosks on the riverbank in any Rhine town. Rail travel along the same route (left bank, via Koblenz) covers the same scenery in half the time if schedule matters.
New for 2026
Added August 2026
Germany-wide — DB Navigator app updated for international travelers
The DB Navigator app (iOS and Android) now fully supports international credit and debit cards for ticket purchase, and has improved English-language support throughout the booking flow. Sparpreis tickets bought in the app are digital and require no printing. The app shows real-time platform information and train delays — more reliable than station departure boards for connecting journeys. If a connection is missed due to DB delay, the app automatically suggests alternative routes and the original ticket remains valid on the later train. Download before arrival in Germany.
Berlin — Reichstag visitor service point relocated
From 14 August 2026, the walk-in Service-Außenstelle for last-minute Reichstag dome registration operates from a new, permanent position directly on Platz der Republik, in front of the Reichstag building. This is the desk to use if you did not register online in advance — it can book leftover slots from as little as 2 hours ahead (subject to availability, and typically no more than 2 days out). Bring a valid photo ID; every visitor aged 16+ needs one regardless of registration method.
Munich — MVV CityTourCard for visitors
The Munich MVV (transit authority) sells a CityTourCard combining unlimited MVV travel with discounts at 100+ tourist partners (museums, tours, restaurants). It comes in three zone options — Zone M (city of Munich), Zone M-6 (adds the surrounding region including the airport, Starnberg, Ammersee, Dachau, Bad Tölz), and Zone M-12 (adds the Alpine foothills: Schliersee, Tegernsee, Chiemsee, Rosenheim, even Kufstein in Austria) — with durations from 24 hours up to 6 days, single or group (up to 5 people). Current prices are best checked at purchase, since MVV updates its fare tables through the year: buy via the MVV app, mvv-muenchen.de, or ticket machines at any S-/U-Bahn station. For a single one-way airport transfer without the card, the S1/S8 airport S-Bahn to central Munich takes about 40 minutes.
Berlin — Brandenburg Gate area visitor management
Berlin has introduced pedestrian management measures around the Brandenburg Gate in 2025–2026, specifically on summer weekend afternoons between Pariser Platz and Tiergarten. Guided group tours of more than 20 people now require a permit in the immediate Brandenburg Gate precinct. For independent visitors, there is no restriction — you can access the gate freely at any time. The measures specifically target the largest commercial tour groups, which had created effectively impassable conditions on the pavement on peak Saturdays.
Bavaria-wide — Schlösser Bavaria combination ticket
The Bayerische Schlösserverwaltung (Bavarian Palace Administration) sells an annual/multi-day combination ticket covering dozens of Bavarian palaces and gardens (Würzburg Residence, Herrenchiemsee, Nymphenburg, Linderhof, and more) — note that Neuschwanstein still requires its own separate timed-entry booking in addition to any combination pass. For visitors planning Bavaria-focused itineraries visiting 3+ palaces, a combination ticket can offer real savings. Check current terms and pricing at schloesser.bayern.de before relying on a specific figure, as bundled-ticket pricing is adjusted periodically.
Seasonal Alerts
2026 Calendar
!
Neuschwanstein — July and August at absolute peak The castle receives 1.5 million visitors annually, with July–August accounting for a disproportionate share. Interior tour slots in these months sell out 3–5 weeks in advance. The bus from Hohenschwangau village to the castle runs every 20 minutes but queues at peak times reach 45 minutes. The 35-minute walk from the Ticket Centre is often faster. Arrive at the first morning slot (09:00) or last afternoon slot (17:00) for the shortest queues.
!
Oktoberfest, Munich — 19 September to 4 October 2026 Munich’s Oktoberfest is the world’s largest folk festival. During the 16-day period, all Munich accommodation is 3–5× the normal price and is booked out a year ahead. If your dates overlap unintentionally: book accommodation by February at the latest or base in Augsburg (30 min by RE), Nuremberg (1h by ICE), or Salzburg (1h 30m) and commute in. The festival itself is free to enter — the tents charge for beer only (€14.50–15 per Maß in 2026). Tent reservations for peak weekend afternoons require a deposit and are effectively non-existent to obtain without a year-ahead booking.
!
German Christmas markets — late November to 24 December Germany’s Christmas markets (Nuremberg, Cologne, Dresden Striezelmarkt, Stuttgart, Hamburg) are genuine and atmospheric — but weekend visits in December are extremely crowded. Markets open typically 26 November to 24 December. Weekday visits before 15:00 are a materially different experience than Saturday evenings in December. Nuremberg market ends at noon on Christmas Eve. Most German businesses and many restaurants close 25–26 December.
!
Rhine Valley — summer Rhine in Flammen (Rhine in Flames) The Rhine in Flames festival series (multiple dates May–September, different sections of the river) involves fireworks, illuminated ships, and large crowds along the riverbank. Hotel prices in Bacharach, Rüdesheim, Koblenz, and Boppard increase significantly on these weekends. If your Rhine Valley visit overlaps with a Rhine in Flames event: book accommodation 3+ months ahead or visit on adjacent non-event nights. Check rhein-in-flammen.com for 2026 dates.
✓
Best windows — May to mid-June and September Germany in late spring (May–June) is at its most beautiful: Rhine Valley vineyards in fresh leaf, Bavaria still uncrowded, Berlin parks and lakes usable, temperatures 16–22°C. September sees Oktoberfest (Munich) but also the quietest post-summer period for almost everywhere else — Rhine Valley golden autumn light, uncrowded Romantic Road towns, and the last warm weeks before the onset of grey autumn. These are the months the guide was written for.
✓
German public holidays — regional variation matters Germany’s 16 federal states have different public holidays beyond the national ones. Bavaria has more public holidays than most states (Corpus Christi, Assumption, All Saints’ Day), affecting museum opening days and local transport. Berlin has its own holiday (3 October, German Unity Day) with city-wide closures. Check feiertage-deutschland.de for your specific destination and travel dates.
