Prices & hours changed

Verified Aug 2026
Athens

Acropolis — Critical update

The guide's stated price of €20 is outdated. The Acropolis has cost €30 year-round since April 2025 — the winter discount was permanently abolished. Timed-entry is mandatory; book online at hhticket.gr. A daily cap of 20,000 visitors is enforced through the online booking system, and slots fill 2–3 weeks ahead in summer.

€20 → €30 hhticket.gr

Athens — Acropolis combo ticket

The €30 combination ticket covering seven archaeological sites was discontinued in April 2025 and, as of August 2026, has not been reinstated. Each site now requires a separate ticket purchase. If you plan to visit the Acropolis plus the Ancient Agora, Roman Agora, or other included sites, budget for individual entry fees — typically €10–20 per site depending on the site's visitor-based category.

€30 combo (7 sites) → Discontinued culture.gr

Athens — Airport metro ticket

The guide states €10 for the airport metro. The correct fare is €9 one-way, €16 return (valid 30 days). The standard €1.20 city ticket does not cover the airport zone — gates reject it. Buy the specific airport ticket at the station.

€10 → €9 oasa.gr

Athens — National Archaeological Museum

Entry price has increased twice since the guide was written. The guide's figure of €12 is well out of date — since 1 January 2026 the ticket costs €20, with no seasonal reduction. EU visitors aged 25 and under remain free with valid ID; seniors (65+, Greek/EU) get 50% off, but only from October to May.

€12 → €20 namuseum.gr

Athens — Acropolis Museum

Previously listed in this guide as unchanged at €10 year-round — that is now the winter price only. Summer admission (April–October) is €15; it drops to €10 for the winter season (November–March). Reduced tickets are €10 in summer and €5 in winter. This is a separate ticket from the hilltop Acropolis site.

€10 (year-round) → €15 summer / €10 winter theacropolismuseum.gr
Crete

Knossos Palace

Entry price has also increased — the guide's €15 is now €20 (effective April 2025, part of the Ministry's new visitor-based site pricing). Summer opening hours remain extended: open until 8:00 PM (last entry 7:45 PM) from April through early September, tapering earlier through autumn. September 8:00–7:30 PM; October 8:00–6:00 PM; November–March 8:30–5:00 PM.

€15 → €20 culture.gr
Peloponnese & mainland

Olympia, Mycenae, Epidaurus, Delphi

The guide's "prices increased slightly to €14" figure was wrong for three of these four sites — the real increases were much larger. Mycenae, Epidaurus and Delphi (site + museum) all now cost €20, not €14. Olympia is the exception and has stayed at €12 (site + museum combined) — it wasn't touched by the Ministry's April 2025 repricing that hit the others. All four still require separate tickets — the old Athens combo never covered them anyway. Delphi remains 2.5–3 hours from Athens by bus or car and deserves a full day, not a rushed half-day stop.

€12 each → Olympia €12 · others €20 culture.gr
Ferries — Piraeus departures

Piraeus to Santorini / Mykonos / Crete — high-speed fares

High-speed ferry fares remain elevated versus the guide's original figures, due to fuel surcharges introduced in 2025. Piraeus–Santorini high-speed: typically €80–110 depending on operator and date. Piraeus–Mykonos high-speed: €70–90. Conventional overnight fares are cheaper and more stable — from around €45–60 to Santorini. Book at openseas.gr, ferryhopper.com, or directly with operators (Seajets, Golden Star, Blue Star).

€70–90 high-speed → €80–110 high-speed openseas.gr

Active scams & warnings

Verified Aug 2026
Athens

Monastiraki & Syntagma metro — Pickpockets

Pickpocket groups operate on crowded metro lines, particularly around Monastiraki and Syntagma stations. Standard precautions: front pockets for phones and wallets, bags worn across the body. Reports have increased since 2024 on the airport line (Line 3) at Syntagma during luggage-laden peak arrivals. Keep valuables in front pockets before boarding, not just when you notice suspicious behaviour.

Athens — "Helpful stranger" metro ticket scam

Someone approaches at the ticket machine offering to help you navigate it, then expects cash payment. Ticket machines have English-language menus — use them yourself. The machines accept contactless cards and cash. No legitimate staff member solicits help payment.

Athens taxis — Meter games

If taking a street taxi (not an app-booked one), confirm the meter is running and set to Tariff 1 (daytime) immediately after entering. Tariff 2 (night rate) applies midnight–5 AM and is legitimate; during daytime it is not. The fixed airport fare is €40 day / €55 night — confirm before departing. Use Uber or FREE NOW (the app formerly known as Beat) for transparent upfront pricing and no cash required.

Athens — Euronet ATMs

Euronet standalone ATMs in tourist areas charge €3–5 per transaction plus unfavourable exchange rates — total cost can reach 8–10% of your withdrawal. Use bank ATMs only: Alpha Bank, Eurobank, Piraeus Bank, National Bank of Greece. These charge no additional fee beyond your home bank's standard foreign transaction fee.

All destinations — Dynamic currency conversion

ATMs and card terminals offer to charge you in your home currency (USD, GBP, AUD). Always refuse. Always select euros. The conversion rate offered is 3–7% worse than your bank's rate. The screen phrases it as "convenience" — it is a hidden fee. This applies at every ATM, restaurant terminal, and hotel checkout in Greece.

Santorini

Santorini — Caldera-view restaurant pricing

Restaurants with direct caldera views in Oia and Fira charge a location premium that can be €15–25 above equivalent food elsewhere on the island. This is not a scam — it is transparent markup for one of the most photographed views in the world. What is worth knowing: the food at caldera-view restaurants is rarely exceptional. The view is the product; the food is incidental. Eat well elsewhere and pay separately for a sunset drink with the view.

All islands

Island restaurant bill padding

Some tourist-area restaurants add items not ordered or inflate prices above menu listings. This is uncommon but documented enough to warrant attention. Check the bill against what you ordered before paying. Fish is priced by the kilogram (€40–70 for common varieties) — always confirm weight and price before the kitchen prepares it, or the bill will surprise you.

New since publication

Added Aug 2026
Athens

Athens — Tap-to-ride now available

Since January 2025, Athens metro and all OASA transport accepts contactless payment directly at gates — tap your credit/debit card, phone, or smartwatch. No need to buy a paper ticket for standard city travel. The system calculates the best fare automatically and applies the daily cap (€4.10) once reached. Airport tickets still require a separate purchase.

Athens — Koukaki neighbourhood

Koukaki has become the clearest alternative to Plaka for eating and staying well at honest prices. Local tavernas on Drakou and Veikou streets serve Athenians, not tourists. Less English on menus, better food on plates, Acropolis views from rooftop terraces at prices below Plaka equivalents. Worth adding to your map if the guide's edition predates this shift.

Cyclades

Naxos — Best Cyclades value in 2026

Naxos has emerged as the strongest value proposition in the Cyclades for 2026 — the largest island, best beaches, most local food culture, and prices 40–50% below Santorini and Mykonos. If the guide was written before Naxos's recent culinary improvement, note that the food scene around Naxos Town has developed significantly since 2023. Mid-range accommodation: €90–150. Taverna dinner: €20–30.

All destinations

All destinations — Climate Crisis Resilience Fee

Since 2024, Greece charges an overnight-stay levy — officially the Climate Crisis Resilience Fee — added to every hotel, apartment, and villa booking, charged per room/unit per night, not per person. For 2026 the rate ranges from €0.50/night (1–2 star properties, low season Nov–Mar) up to €15/night (5-star hotels and larger short-term rentals, high season Apr–Oct); most mid-range 3–4 star stays fall around €3–8/night in summer. It applies island-wide, including Santorini, Mykonos, and Crete, is collected directly by the accommodation (usually payable in cash on arrival or added to the final bill), and is separate from VAT. Budget an extra €20–100+ across a typical stay depending on category and length.

All destinations — ETIAS travel authorisation: still NOT required

Correction to this guide: ETIAS has not launched. It was originally expected in 2024, then repeatedly postponed — most recently the EU dropped its late-2026 target during the summer of 2026, and reporting now points to 2027 with no confirmed date. As of August 2026, non-EU visa-exempt travellers (US, UK, Canada, Australia) can still enter Greece with just a valid passport — no ETIAS, no fee, nothing to apply for yet. When it does launch, the only official channel will be the EU's own domain, travel-europe.europa.eu/etias — the planned fee is €7. Be wary of third-party sites, including the one previously listed here, that invite early registration or charge more; there is nothing legitimate to register for until the EU announces a launch date.
