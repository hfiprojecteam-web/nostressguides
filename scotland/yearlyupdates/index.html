Verified August 2026
Edinburgh — new visitor charge
Edinburgh — Visitor Levy (new tourist tax)
Edinburgh introduced Scotland’s first city-wide Visitor Levy on 24 July 2026 — a mandatory 5% charge on the cost of paid overnight accommodation, calculated before VAT. It applies to hotels, aparthotels, bed and breakfasts, guest houses, hostels, self-catering flats and holiday/short-term lets. The levy is only charged on the first 5 nights of any stay — nights beyond that are levy-free — and it is not charged on parking, meals, drinks or transport. Bookings made and paid for (in part or in full) before 1 October 2025, for stays on or after 24 July 2026, are exempt. The charge is added automatically to your accommodation bill by the property, not collected separately at a checkpoint. Budget an extra 5% on top of your quoted room rate for any Edinburgh stay from summer 2026 onward.
No city-wide accommodation tax (2024–2025) → 5% of accommodation cost, capped at 5 nights (2026) edinburgh.gov.uk
ScotRail — fares and services
ScotRail — peak fares abolished, then frozen for 2026
ScotRail permanently abolished peak-time fares from 1 September 2025 — you now pay the same price at any time of day, on any day of the week, on every route. The change roughly halved costs on the busiest commuter corridors: the Edinburgh–Glasgow Anytime Day Return fell from £32.60 to £16.80. On top of that, the Scottish Government confirmed in February 2026 that all ScotRail fares — including season tickets and the Flexipass — are frozen with no increase for 12 months from 1 April 2026. Advance purchase tickets (booked at least 7 days ahead, tied to a specific train) remain the cheapest option for longer intercity journeys such as Edinburgh–Inverness. ScotRail Railcards (26–30 Card, Disabled Persons, Senior, Two Together) continue to give 34% off.
Edinburgh–Glasgow Anytime Day Return £32.60 (pre-Sept 2025) → £16.80, same price any time (2026) scotrail.co.uk
ScotRail — reduced Sunday services (West Highland Line)
The West Highland Line (Glasgow–Fort William–Mallaig) continues to operate reduced Sunday services on certain sections, with only 2 trains per day in each direction on Sundays between Glasgow and Mallaig. This remains a significant operational point for island-hopping itineraries: visitors connecting from Oban or Fort William to an afternoon CalMac ferry on Sundays must plan with care. Check the Sunday timetable at scotrail.co.uk before booking CalMac crossings. Weekday and Saturday services remain at full frequency.
CalMac ferries — fares and booking
CalMac car ferry bookings remain essential for peak season — some routes fully booked weeks ahead
CalMac Ferries (Caledonian MacBrayne) requires advance booking for all car crossings on the busiest routes (Ullapool–Stornoway, Oban–Mull, Mallaig–Armadale). Car spaces on peak Friday evenings and Sunday afternoons routinely sell out weeks ahead in July and August. Book at calmac.co.uk as soon as your dates are confirmed — the booking window opens roughly 12 weeks before departure. Foot passengers can almost always travel without a booking, but car drivers arriving without a reservation on peak days will be turned away. Off-peak crossings (Monday–Thursday, most October–April sailings) remain easier to book close to travel.
CalMac — passenger fare increases, Summer 2026 timetable
CalMac fares rose by 3.4% (in line with CPI inflation measured May 2025), effective with the Summer 2026 timetable running 27 March–18 October 2026. The Ullapool–Stornoway foot passenger fare is now £12.75 each way (£25.50 return). Car crossings increased proportionally — a single car crossing on this route is now £69.05. The Island Hopscotch tickets (pre-purchased multi-ferry passes) remain the best value for visitors planning to visit multiple islands — check calmac.co.uk for the Hopscotch options covering different island combinations.
Ullapool–Stornoway foot passenger from £12.30 → £12.75 (2026 summer timetable) calmac.co.uk
Major attraction changes
Edinburgh — Edinburgh Castle
Edinburgh Castle adult (16–64) entry is now £23.50 online / £26.00 if bought at the gate (up from £17.50 in 2024). The castle remains Scotland’s most-visited paid attraction. Key change: timed entry slots remain mandatory for all visitors — you cannot arrive without a pre-booked time slot, even with a Historic Environment Scotland Explorer Pass. Slots go on sale on a rolling window. Book at edinburghcastle.scot. Also changed: entry is no longer automatically free for accompanied under-16s — a paid Child ticket now applies to ages 7–15 (£14.00 online / £15.50 at the gate); check the site for current age bands before budgeting a family visit. A Flexi Ticket (£38.00 adult) is now available, giving one entry on any day within a 7-day window with no fixed timeslot to book — useful if your exact visit day is uncertain. The Crown Jewels (Honours of Scotland) and Stone of Destiny remain included in all adult tickets.
Adult entry £17.50 (2024) → £23.50 online / £26.00 gate (2026) edinburghcastle.scot
Skye — Old Man of Storr parking now tiered by duration
The Old Man of Storr car park charge has moved from a flat per-visit fee to a tiered structure: £6 for up to 6 hours, £7 for up to 12 hours, payable by coins, contactless card or parking app at the machines on site. The hike itself and the mountain remain completely free to access. Visitors arriving by Stagecoach bus (route 57A from Portree) pay nothing and are set down at the car park entrance. The car park (roughly 140 spaces) still fills by 9:30am on peak summer days — arrive before 9am or after 3pm when visitors begin leaving. Do not scan any QR code on parking signage or machines to pay — official guidance warns these are used for parking scams; pay only via the physical machine or an official app.
Flat £3 per vehicle (2025) → £6 (up to 6h) / £7 (up to 12h) (2026) on-site signage / operator
Entry requirements
UK ETA — Non-visa nationals from the EU, US, Canada, Australia
The UK Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) has been strictly and fully enforced since 25 February 2026 for visitors from visa-exempt countries including the EU, US, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand. Apply at gov.uk/apply-eta before travel — the ETA costs £20, is valid for 2 years or until your passport expires (whichever comes first), and covers multiple visits of up to 6 months each. Application takes approximately 10 minutes online and is typically approved within minutes, though it does not guarantee entry. Every traveller needs their own ETA, including babies and children. The ETA is linked to your passport electronically — you do not receive a physical stamp or document. EU citizens no longer need to show an ID card for entry to the UK; a passport is required. Beware third-party websites that charge more than the official £20 fee — apply only via gov.uk.

Urquhart Castle (Loch Ness) — Historic Environment Scotland site Updated for 2026: adult entry is now £14.00 online / £16.00 at the gate (concession £11.00/£13.00, child 7–15 £8.50/£9.50). Edinburgh Fringe Festival — 7–31 August 2026 Edinburgh’s Fringe is the world’s largest arts festival; the 2026 edition runs 7–31 August. During the festival, Edinburgh accommodation costs 3–5× the normal price and books out months ahead. If your dates overlap: book accommodation by March at the latest, or base in Glasgow or Stirling (45–50 min by ScotRail) and commute in. Free shows on the Royal Mile run all day — the paid Fringe shows range from £7–25 and must be booked at edfringe.com. Exact dates shift by a few days each year — always confirm on edfringe.com before booking travel.
!
Midge season — May to September (peak June–August) Highland midges (Culicoides impunctatus) are small biting insects that emerge in calm, warm, humid conditions from late May. They are most intense at dawn and dusk, particularly near standing water. Smidge repellent (smidgeup.com) is the most effective product available — DEET-based repellents are less effective against Scottish midges. Wind disperses them immediately; the midge forecast at midgeforecast.co.uk updates daily. Midges do not carry disease.
✓
Best windows — May to mid-June and September Scotland in late spring (May–June) offers long days (17+ hours of daylight), wildflowers, and uncrowded Highlands. September has golden highland light, fewer midges than summer, and post-peak prices. These are the months the guide was written for. July and August are busiest (and midgiest) but have the most reliable ferry availability and the longest daylight.

Added August 2026
Transport
Scotland — ScotRail Flexipass (restructured)
ScotRail’s Flexipass was restructured in 2025–2026: it is now 12 single journeys (up from 10) on your chosen route, usable on any day within a 60-day window, saving at least 5% compared to buying six full-price return tickets — and up to 20–60% on some routes. Important: this is a route-specific commuter product, not an all-network unlimited-travel pass. It suits visitors making repeated trips along one line (e.g. staying in Edinburgh and doing several day trips to Glasgow) rather than a single multi-region tour. Pricing depends on the route selected. For visitors touring several different lines in one trip (Highlands, islands, cities), booking individual Advance singles per journey is usually more cost-effective than a Flexipass. Buy at scotrail.co.uk/tickets/flexipass.
Scotland — Caledonian Sleeper (Edinburgh/Glasgow–London)
The Caledonian Sleeper (caledoniansleeper.com) runs nightly between London Euston and Edinburgh, Glasgow, Inverness, Fort William, and Aberdeen. A seated ticket from London to Edinburgh remains a budget option that eliminates a night’s accommodation cost; cabin berths include a continental breakfast. Book at caledoniansleeper.com and check current fares directly, as sleeper pricing moves with demand. The Fort William sleeper arrives at the foot of Ben Nevis — an extraordinary arrival for hillwalkers.
Highlands and islands
NC500 — North Coast 500 road trip planning
The NC500 (northcoast500.com) is Scotland’s 516-mile coastal driving route around the northern Highlands, starting and ending in Inverness. Key planning notes: (1) Most NC500 accommodation books out by March for July and August — book immediately once dates are confirmed. (2) The route is most enjoyable anti-clockwise (Inverness–east coast–north coast–west coast return) as it puts you on the scenic side approaching Torridon and Assynt. (3) Campervan rental from Inverness covers accommodation and transport simultaneously — sites at Torridon, Dunnet Bay, and Scourie are excellent and take campervans. (4) The NC500 is realistically 5 days minimum; 7 days is unhurried.
Skye — Fairy Pools parking and timing
The Fairy Pools at Glenbrittle (south Skye, 30 min from Portree) are one of Scotland’s busiest attractions. The car park has an overflow facility but still fills early in July and August — check current parking charges on site, as pricing structures for Skye car parks have been revised in 2025–2026 (see the Old Man of Storr update above for the pattern). The pools themselves are at their most beautiful in the morning light (south-facing). Correct approach: arrive before 8:30am, or come after 5pm when most tour groups have left. Wild swimming in the pools is permitted and popular — the water is cold year-round (8–14°C). The walk from the car park to the first pool is 20 minutes on a maintained path.
Money and practicalities
Scotland — Scottish banknotes and card acceptance
Scottish banknotes (issued by Royal Bank of Scotland, Bank of Scotland, and Clydesdale Bank) are legal currency but not legal tender — they are widely accepted across Scotland but can occasionally be refused by businesses in England. You cannot easily use Scottish banknotes in England — spend them in Scotland or exchange at a bank before crossing the border. Contactless and chip-and-pin card payment is now accepted almost universally across Scotland, including remote areas. However, some Outer Hebrides and small island businesses remain cash-preferred — carry £50–100 in cash for island visits. There are ATMs in Stornoway, Portree, and Tobermory, but none on smaller islands (Iona, Colonsay, Rum, Eigg).
Edinburgh — budgeting for the Visitor Levy
From 24 July 2026, remember to add 5% of your quoted Edinburgh accommodation cost (capped at 5 nights) when comparing prices against other Scottish cities or against your 2025 budget expectations — the levy is charged on top of the room rate, before VAT, and is not usually shown in headline search prices on booking platforms until checkout. Edinburgh Fringe — 7–31 August 2026 World’s largest arts festival. All Edinburgh accommodation 3–5× normal price during the festival — and, from this year, the Edinburgh Visitor Levy (5%) applies on top. Book by March or base in Glasgow (45 min by ScotRail). Free Royal Mile shows run all day — paid shows from £7 at edfringe.com. The opening weekend of the Fringe has the largest street crowds of the entire festival.

Highland Games season — June to September Traditional Highland Games (caber tossing, heavy athletics, pipe bands, Highland dancing) take place across Scotland June–September. The Braemar Gathering (September, near Balmoral) is the most famous and typically attended by the Royal Family. Games at Balloch, Dunoon, Oban, and Inveraray also draw large crowds. Nearby accommodation books out for Braemar 4–6 months ahead. Check the Highland Games calendar at shga.co.uk for exact 2026/27 dates.

Hogmanay (New Year) — Edinburgh, 29 December 2026–1 January 2027 Edinburgh’s Hogmanay is Europe’s largest New Year celebration, drawing around 45,000 people to the Street Party alone. The programme opens with the Torchlight Procession (29 December), continues with the Night Afore concert on 30 December, then the Street Party, concert and midnight fireworks on Hogmanay itself (31 December), with the First Footin’ culture trail on New Year’s Day. The Street Party is ticketed (from £30, edinburghshogmanay.com); the Loony Dook (New Year’s Day sea swim at South Queensferry) remains free to watch. Edinburgh accommodation on 31 December is the most expensive night of the year — book 6+ months ahead or base in Glasgow and check the ScotRail holiday-period timetable.

Winter road closures — A835, A832, and NC500 sections Several Highland roads are subject to snow closures November–March, including sections of the A835 (Ullapool route), A832 (Torridon), and the north coast sections of the NC500. The A9 (Perth–Inverness) has mandatory winter tyre requirements on certain sections. Check road conditions at trafficscotland.org before any Highland drive in winter. ScotRail West Highland Line remains open year-round but may experience weather delays.

Whisky distillery visitor centre opening hours — reduced in winter Most Speyside distilleries reduce visitor centre hours significantly between November and March. Glenfiddich (open year-round daily), Macallan (closed Sundays November–March), and Balvenie (reduced tours, pre-booking essential) all operate shortened winter schedules. Check individual distillery websites before planning a winter Speyside visit.
✓
Best midge-free window — September September combines the best of Scottish travel: low midge activity, golden highland light, uncrowded attractions, lower accommodation prices than July–August, and the CalMac ferry routes still running at full frequency. The weather is unpredictable but the light quality on clear September days (particularly in Skye and the Outer Hebrides) is exceptional. This is the month the guide was written for.
