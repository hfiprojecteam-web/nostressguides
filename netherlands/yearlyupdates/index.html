Amsterdam — tourist tax
Amsterdam tourist tax is 12.5% of the room price, not a flat fee — and a further rise is already approved for 2027
Amsterdam's overnight tourist tax (toeristenbelasting) is 12.5% of the overnight accommodation price (excl. VAT) — confirmed unchanged for 2026. This is a percentage, not a fixed per-person fee: a €150/night hotel room adds roughly €18.75 in tax, a €250/night room roughly €31.25. A couple staying 4 nights at a mid-range €180/night hotel pays around €90 in tourist tax across the stay — the total scales with what you book, so it pays to check the tax line on your booking confirmation rather than budgeting a flat nightly figure. Rotterdam's equivalent (logiesbelasting) is 6.5% of the room price; Utrecht and Haarlem charge less. The city council has already approved a further increase from 2027: the rate rises to 16%, then an extra percentage point every year after that — so the direction of travel is firmly upward.

NS increased all domestic train fares by 6.52% on 1 January 2026 — the steepest rise in years, combining a scheduled increase with a deferred increase carried over from prior years. Amsterdam – Haarlem €5.50. Amsterdam – Leiden €12. Amsterdam – Delft €17.30. Amsterdam – Maastricht €33.30. Schiphol Airport — train to Amsterdam Centraal
The Schiphol Airport train supplement (a “Schiphol supplement” charged in addition to your standard ticket) now means a single journey from Schiphol to Amsterdam Centraal costs €5.50 with OV-Pay contactless tap-in. Journey time: 15–18 minutes, trains every 10 minutes daytime. Always tap in AND tap out — failing to tap out charges the maximum fare for the route. The NS International desk at Schiphol Arrival Hall 3 sells day passes, but for most visitors the OV-Pay tap-on-tap-off contactless bank card is the simplest option.

Attractions — key price and access changes
Amsterdam — Anne Frank House
The Anne Frank House operates a fully mandatory advance-booking system with no walk-up tickets available — not now, not during low season, not on a Tuesday morning in November. Adult price: €16.50 (includes a €1 booking fee); ages 10–17: €7; ages 0–9: €1 (a ticket is required at every age, including infants). Book at annefrank.org. New tickets are released every Tuesday at 10:00 CEST, for a visit six weeks later — this replaced the old “2 months ahead at 09:00” release pattern. Peak-season slots (March–October, all weekends) go within minutes of release. Set a reminder for Tuesday mornings once your dates are fixed — this is the single most commonly missed booking in Amsterdam. Tickets are non-transferable and non-refundable.
€14 (book) → €16.50, released Tue 10am CEST for a visit 6 weeks out (2026) annefrank.org
Amsterdam — Rijksmuseum
Rijksmuseum adult entry increased to €25. Online advance booking is strongly recommended — the museum does not guarantee same-day walk-up entry during busy periods and queues on weekends can exceed 45 minutes. Book at rijksmuseum.nl. Permanent collection highlights: Rembrandt’s Night Watch, Vermeer’s The Milkmaid, and the largest collection of 17th-century Dutch master paintings in the world. Garden and library reading room remain free.
€20 (book) → €25 (2026) rijksmuseum.nl
Amsterdam — Van Gogh Museum
Van Gogh Museum adult entry is now €25 (students with a valid card: €16; visitors under 18: free). Mandatory advance booking at vangoghmuseum.nl. No walk-up entry available on weekends or during school holidays. Timed-entry slots book out 3–4 weeks ahead in spring and summer. The museum holds the world’s largest Van Gogh collection: 200 paintings, 500 drawings. Allow 2 hours minimum. The permanent collection is chronological — the Potato Eaters period is on level 0, the sunflowers on level 1.
€20 (book) → €25 (2026) vangoghmuseum.nl
Lisse — Keukenhof Gardens
Keukenhof's 2026 season (19 March–17 May) has already closed for the year. The 2027 season runs 18 March–9 May 2027, and ticket sales reopen at keukenhof.nl from mid-November 2026 — 2026-season pricing was around €21–22 online (€25 at the gate), and 2027 pricing had not yet been published at the time of this check. Advance booking is mandatory whenever the park is open — there is no walk-up ticket sales at the gate. Combine entry with Bus 858 from Schiphol (included in some ticket bundles) or Bus 854 from Leiden Centraal. Peak bloom typically falls in the last two weeks of April: in 2026, King’s Day (27 April) coincided with near-peak tulips and the season’s highest visitor numbers — expect a similar pattern in 2027. If Keukenhof is a priority for your 2027 trip, aim for a weekday visit in the last week of April and book the moment tickets go live in November.
€19 (book) → €21–22 (2026 season, now closed); 2027 price TBA keukenhof.nl
The Hague — Mauritshuis: temporary closure and Monday hours
Two corrections to what most guides say about the Mauritshuis. First, it is not closed on Mondays — it simply opens later that day, at 13:00 instead of the usual 10:00 (Tuesday–Sunday: 10:00–18:00). Second, and more urgent for anyone travelling soon: the museum is fully closed to the public from 24 August to 20 September 2026 for maintenance work. It reopens after 20 September. Adult admission is now around €25.50, with a new reduced €4 ticket for the last two hours of the day (16:00–18:00) introduced 1 January 2026, currently limited to Dutch residents. Always check mauritshuis.nl before planning a visit.

Entry requirements
ETIAS — US, UK, Canadian, Australian travelers
The book correctly notes that ETIAS has been delayed repeatedly. As of August 2026, ETIAS is still not in force for the Netherlands or any Schengen country. The official target had been the last quarter of 2026, but reporting through the first half of 2026 points to further slippage into 2027. Check current requirements at etiasvisa.com or the EU’s official travel portal (travel-europe.europa.eu) before departure. When it launches: fully online, approximately €7, valid three years. Not a visa — a pre-travel authorisation similar to the US ESTA. Non-EU citizens who currently enter the Netherlands without a visa will need ETIAS once active.

OV-Pay — contactless bank card works at all major NS station gates OV-Pay (tap your Visa or Mastercard) is now accepted at virtually all NS train stations, GVB metro and tram stops in Amsterdam, and RET in Rotterdam. Tap in at the gate when boarding, tap out when leaving. Charged within 24 hours at the standard NS second-class fare. No registration required. This is now the most practical option for visitors who prefer not to buy an OV-chipkaart or the new OV-pas.

NS Dal (off-peak) discount — approximately 40% off peak fares Confirmed. Off-peak travel (Mon–Fri departures after 09:00 and before 16:00, plus all day Sat–Sun) remains significantly cheaper. On a leisure itinerary, scheduling trains after 09:00 on weekdays saves meaningfully across a full trip. Tap OV-Pay or check “dal” fare at ns.nl.

GVB Amsterdam — 24h pass €10, 48h €16, 72h €21.50 Confirmed 2026 rates (GVB Day Ticket and GVB Multi-day Ticket, gvb.nl). Covers all GVB metro, tram, and bus lines within Amsterdam. Does not cover NS trains or regional buses outside the city. Buy at any GVB ticket machine, the GVB app, or the Visitors Centre on Stationsplein. Validate before boarding every tram and bus (tap the card reader).

Free GVB ferry — Amsterdam Centraal rear exit to Noord Confirmed free. The GVB passenger ferries from Amsterdam Centraal (rear of station, IJ-side) to Amsterdam Noord depart every 5–7 minutes, 24 hours a day, at no charge. No ticket, no card tap required. The NDSM ferry (Pier 15) reaches the creative district in 15 minutes, also free. Do not pay for tourist canal cruises to reach Noord.

Stedelijk Museum, Amsterdam — €22.50 Confirmed. The Netherlands’ premier modern and contemporary art museum. Mondrian, Malevich, Appel, Warhol, and the largest Mondrian collection in the world. New for 2026
Added August 2026
Amsterdam — I amsterdam City Card discontinued; Museum Card recommended instead
The old I amsterdam City Card (which included transport) has been replaced by a restructured pass system. For museum-focused visitors, the Museumkaart (annual card, now €75 for adults / €39 for visitors up to 18) remains the best value if visiting 3+ major museums in a year: covers Rijksmuseum, Van Gogh Museum, Stedelijk, Mauritshuis, and 500+ others across the Netherlands. Non-residents can buy a trial month card at any participating museum. The Anne Frank House is not included and requires separate booking regardless of which card you hold. Confirm current pass terms at museumkaart.nl before purchase.
Netherlands-wide — OV-chipkaart being phased out; OVpay and the new OV-pas take over
NS and the main city transit operators have accelerated the rollout of OVpay contactless bank card payment. From 1 July 2026, new anonymous OV-chipkaarts can no longer be purchased (existing cards keep working for now), and the whole OV-chipkaart system is due to be fully retired by the end of 2027, replaced by OVpay (tap your bank card) and the new OV-pas — a chip-card successor, about €6, sold at NS ticket machines and Primera shops. For visitors this changes little in practice: OVpay remains the simplest option. Tap your Visa/Mastercard at the yellow card reader when boarding NS trains, GVB trams and metros, and RET metro. The main practical point to remember: OVpay requires tapping out to close the journey — if you forget, the system charges the maximum route fare. Set a reminder on your phone for your first few journeys until tapping out becomes automatic.
Amsterdam — city’s 2026 visitor management measures
Amsterdam has expanded several visitor management measures in 2026. The most significant for travelers: cruise ship arrivals have been rerouted away from the city centre (docking now at NDSM or IJmuiden instead of Central Station pier), meaningfully reducing morning pedestrian pressure in the canal district. Additionally, the city has banned new short-stay rental licences in the historic centre — Airbnb supply is constrained, pushing prices higher and booking windows longer. Plan accommodation 3–4 months ahead for summer travel.
Netherlands-wide — NS Flex subscription for longer stays
For stays of a week or more making frequent rail journeys, NS Flex is worth considering. Entry-level NS Flex Dal Voordeel (from €6.35/month) gives roughly 40% off in the daluren (off-peak hours); higher tiers such as NS Flex Dal Vrij (from €127.95/month) add unlimited free travel during off-peak hours, and NS Flex Altijd Vrij (from €399.95/month) covers unlimited travel at any time. Available at ns.nl. Requires a Dutch bank account or payment method for the subscription — check current availability for non-residents. Shorter-stay visitors are better served by OVpay and simply travelling off-peak.
Amsterdam — cannabis coffeeshop tourist policy remains unsettled
Amsterdam introduced a ban on tourists from cannabis coffeeshops in the city centre from May 2025 onward, covering most tourist neighbourhoods including the Jordaan, Centrum, and Red Light District. Since then, Dutch reporting through the first half of 2026 shows the policy has been actively revisited by the city council following the March 2026 local elections, with the practical situation on the ground varying and reported inconsistently across sources at the time of this check. We were not able to confirm a single, stable answer for August 2026 — if a coffeeshop visit is part of your plans, confirm the current policy directly at amsterdam.nl or with your accommodation immediately before travel. The rest of the city’s culture — its museums, food, cycling, architecture, and canal life — is entirely unaffected either way.
Seasonal Alerts
2026–2027 Calendar
!
King’s Day — 27 April The Netherlands’ largest national celebration turns the entire country, and Amsterdam in particular, into one enormous outdoor party. Millions descend on Amsterdam for the flea market, orange clothing, and street concerts. Accommodation in Amsterdam is fully booked months in advance and prices are 3–5× normal. If your dates overlap: either book 4+ months ahead and embrace the chaos, or base in Rotterdam, Utrecht, or Haarlem and join Amsterdam for the day. Trains run but are extremely crowded.
!
Keukenhof — 18 March to 9 May 2027 (2026 season already closed), and peak bloom pressure Keukenhof receives around 1.4 million visitors each season. The final two weeks of April are consistently peak bloom and peak crowds together. Weekends during this window are extremely busy. If tulips are the goal for a 2027 trip: a weekday visit in the last week of April is peak colour with manageable crowds. Ticket sales for the 2027 season open at keukenhof.nl from mid-November 2026 — book tickets and transport together; parking is a serious problem, so the bus from Leiden or Schiphol is the correct approach.
!
Dutch school holidays — July and August crush Dutch summer holidays run approximately late June through late August. Amsterdam, Giethoorn, Zaanse Schans, and Kinderdijk all operate at absolute capacity on summer weekends. July and August weekday visits are manageable; weekend visits require very early arrival (before 10:00) or are best avoided at peak sites. September sees an immediate and significant drop in visitor volume with temperatures still warm.
!
Dutch Design Week, Eindhoven — late October (18–26 October 2026) Eindhoven’s Dutch Design Week is Europe’s largest design event. Hotels in Eindhoven book out entirely. If your itinerary includes Eindhoven during this window, book accommodation 3+ months ahead or base in ’s-Hertogenbosch or Tilburg (30 minutes by train). The week is excellent if planned for; accommodation is genuinely impossible to find last minute.
!
Giethoorn — summer weekends, operational disruption Giethoorn (the “Venice of the Netherlands”) has 2,500 residents and receives 1.5 million annual visitors, the large majority concentrated on summer weekends. The village has introduced informal visitor dispersal measures and the canals can become genuinely gridlocked with hire boats on Saturday afternoons in July and August. Weekday visits in May or September are a different experience entirely. Bus 70 from Steenwijk takes 25 minutes.
✓
Best windows — mid-April to late May and September–October Spring in the Netherlands peaks in the last two weeks of April: tulips at or near peak bloom, temperatures 12–18°C, long golden-hour light, canals in full colour. September and October are the locals’ preferred months: summer crowds are gone, temperatures remain warm (16–20°C in September), and museum queues halve. These are the months the guide was written for.
✓
Amsterdam Light Festival — late November through January The Amsterdam Light Festival runs along the Amstel and canal ring from late November through mid-January. Light art installations illuminate the canals and bridge crossings. Free to view on foot; ticketed boat cruises also available. One of Amsterdam’s most atmospheric winter experiences, and one of the few that actually benefits from shorter days and earlier darkness. Check dates and map at amsterdamlightfestival.com.
