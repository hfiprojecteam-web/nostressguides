What Changed
Verified March 2026
Brussels Airport — train and taxi
Brussels Airport train: Diabolo surcharge now €6.10 — action required
The Belgian rail network charges a mandatory Diabolo supplement on all train journeys to and from Brussels Airport (Zaventem). This surcharge increased to €6.10 in January 2026 (up from €4.80). It is charged on top of your standard SNCB ticket and applies automatically when you purchase a ticket to or from the airport. A standard adult Brussels Airport → Brussels-Central now costs approximately €14.90 total including the Diabolo fee. The surcharge is not covered by the Go Pass or Rail Pass — you pay it separately regardless of which pass you hold.
SNCB/NMBS — standard domestic fares
Belgian domestic train fares increased by approximately 5.9% on 1 February 2026, in line with Belgium’s automatic cost-of-living indexation. Key fares: Brussels–Bruges now €15.90, Brussels–Ghent €8.70, Brussels–Antwerp €8.70, Brussels–Liège €13, Brussels–Namur €11. The Rail Pass for adults 26+ (10 journeys, 2nd class) is now €97. The Go Pass 10 for under-26 travelers is now €57. All current fares confirmed at sncb.be.
Bxl–Bruges: €15 (book) → €15.90 (2026) sncb.be
Brussels Airport — regulated taxi flat rate
The regulated flat-rate taxi from Brussels Airport to central Brussels is now €55 for up to four passengers with standard luggage. Use only taxis with the official blue-and-yellow “Brussels Airport Taxi” livery, from the official rank on Level -1 of the terminal. Unlicensed drivers inside the arrivals hall routinely charge €80–120 for the same journey. If approached inside the building, decline and proceed to the official rank. The SNCB Airport Express train remains faster and cheaper at peak hours.
€45–50 (book) → €55 regulated (2026) brusselsairport.be
Attractions — key price and access changes
Bruges — Belfry (Belfort)
The Bruges Belfry now operates on a mandatory timed-ticket system. Walk-up tickets are no longer sold at the door during April–October. Book online at ticket.bruges.be in advance. Adult price: €16. Capacity is strictly controlled to reduce congestion on the 366-step spiral staircase. In July–August, slots fill 3–5 days ahead. Book the Belfry before anything else when planning your Bruges visit.
€14 (book) → €16 + advance booking required (2026) ticket.bruges.be
Ghent — The Ghent Altarpiece, Sint-Baafskathedraal
The Ghent Altarpiece (Van Eyck, 1432) now requires separate timed-entry booking to the dedicated chapel within Sint-Baafskathedraal. General cathedral access remains free. Viewing the Altarpiece costs €6 and must be booked at sintbaafskathedraal.be. Peak season weekend slots sell out. Walk-up availability exists on weekday mornings in low season only. The book’s reference to free access is no longer accurate.
Free with cathedral access (book) → €6, advance booking required (2026) sintbaafskathedraal.be
Brussels — Atomium
Atomium adult entry increased to €18. Online booking is strongly recommended — the Atomium sells out on weekends and public holidays. Book at atomium.be. The price covers all currently open spheres and the panoramic top sphere. Combination tickets with adjacent Mini-Europe offer better value if visiting both.
€16 (book) → €18 (2026) atomium.be
Entry requirements
ETIAS — US, UK, Canadian, Australian travelers
The book correctly notes ETIAS has been delayed repeatedly. As of early 2026, ETIAS is still not in force for Belgium or any Schengen country. Check current requirements at etiasvisa.com or the EU’s official travel portal before departure. When it launches: fully online, approximately €7, valid three years. Not a visa — a pre-travel authorization similar to the US ESTA. Non-EU citizens who currently enter Belgium without a visa will need ETIAS once active.

New for 2026
Added March 2026
Bruges — visitor management measures in force
Bruges has introduced light crowd-management measures in the historic centre during peak season (July–August and holiday weekends). These include pedestrian flow guidance at Markt, Rozenhoedkaai, and Burg, and occasional flow caps on the Belfry staircase. No formal daily visitor cap on the city itself exists yet. The practical effect: arriving in Bruges on a Saturday in August without pre-booked accommodation and a Belfry ticket means a very different experience than described in the book. Weekday visits in May, June, September, or October remain unhurried.
Antwerp — Antwerp City Card (new for 2026)
Antwerp has launched a consolidated Antwerp City Card covering free entry to 20+ museums including MAS, Rubens House, Museum Plantin-Moretus, and KMSKA (Royal Museum of Fine Arts), plus unlimited city tram and bus. Available in 24h (€29), 48h (€39), and 72h (€49) versions at visitantwerpen.be. Worth calculating: Rubens House (€12) + MAS (€12) + one further museum already covers the 24h card price.
Belgium-wide — SNCB Weekend Ticket confirmed
SNCB has confirmed the continuation of the Weekend Ticket: a return journey purchased on Saturday, Sunday, or a public holiday costs the same as a single one-way fare — a 50% return discount. Buy at any station on the day or at sncb.be. This is one of the best-value transport deals in Europe and is frequently overlooked by visitors. Brussels → Bruges return on a Saturday: €15.90 instead of €31.80.
Brussels — STIB Noctis night network expanded
Brussels STIB has expanded its Noctis night bus network. Noctis buses now run Friday and Saturday nights from approximately 00:30 to 05:00, covering all main nightlife areas and connecting to the main stations. Standard STIB day passes are not valid on Noctis lines — a flat-rate Noctis ticket (€3) is required per journey, bought on board. Route maps at stib.be under “Night bus.”
Belgium-wide — contactless payment rolling out on SNCB
SNCB is rolling out contactless bank card (Visa/Mastercard) tap-to-pay at gates in major stations throughout 2026. Operational at Brussels-Midi, Brussels-Central, Brussels-Nord, Antwerp-Centraal, and Ghent-Sint-Pieters as of March 2026. Tapping a bank card charges a single full-price ticket — no Rail Pass or Go Pass discount applies. Use the SNCB app or physical pass for discounted journeys. Bank card tap is for single ticket purchases only.
Seasonal Alerts
2026 Calendar
!
Bruges summer weekends — July and August Bruges receives over 8 million day-trippers a year in a historic centre roughly 1km across. Saturday and Sunday afternoons in July–August are genuinely unpleasant at the Markt and Rozenhoedkaai. Hotel prices peak and availability disappears months ahead. Arrive Friday evening, leave Sunday morning before noon — or choose Ghent instead: equally beautiful, half the crowd, 30 minutes by IC.
!
Gentse Feesten — third week of July Ten days of free open-air concerts across Ghent’s entire historic centre. Over a million visitors. Hotels book out a year ahead. If your dates overlap: book accommodation months in advance or base in Bruges or Brussels (30–35 min by IC). Spectacular if planned for; logistically impossible if not.
!
Belgian National Day — 21 July Public holiday. Military parade and celebrations in Brussels. Most shops and some museums close. Transport runs on Sunday schedule. Brussels hotels around July 21 book early — verify availability if your dates overlap.
!
Formula 1 Belgian Grand Prix — late August, Spa-Francorchamps Hotels within 60km of the circuit fill completely. Liège, Namur, and Brussels see elevated prices that weekend. Tickets at spa-francorchamps.be open a year ahead. If your dates overlap unintentionally: book accommodation early or accept significantly higher rates.
!
Belgian school holidays — rolling crowds Belgium’s Flemish, French-speaking, and German-speaking communities stagger their breaks slightly. Major attractions and coastal areas see rolling surges through February, Easter, July–August, and autumn half-term. Check exact dates at belgium.be against your travel window.
✓
Best windows — May to mid-June and September–October Spring in Belgium is consistently excellent: full museum programs, 14–20°C, manageable crowds. October brings Ardennes autumn colours, quieter Bruges, and harvest-season Wallonian food. These are the months the guide was written for.
✓
Christmas markets — late November through 5 January Belgium runs some of northern Europe’s finest Christmas markets. Brussels’ Winter Wonders on Grand Place is the largest. Bruges on the Markt is atmospheric but crowded. Ghent runs a quieter local market. Liège (one of Europe’s longest-running, since 1989) is excellent and significantly less touristy. Early December weekday visits give you the atmosphere without the worst crowds.
