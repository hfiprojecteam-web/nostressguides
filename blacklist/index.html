Restaurants to avoid

Updated 2026

Rome

Within 200 metres of the Colosseum

The entire perimeter

No restaurant within a 200-metre radius of the Colosseum represents good value. This is not an exaggeration — it is a documented, consistent pattern. Average pasta dish: €18–26. Average quality: below a supermarket ready meal. The area exists exclusively to extract money from people who haven't eaten since the airport.

Red flags

Menu photos on a board outside. Waiter standing in the doorway actively inviting you in. "Tourist menu" sign. Prices not visible before you sit. Any of these: walk past.

Piazza Navona & Campo de' Fiori — all restaurants facing the square

Beautiful square, terrible food

Every restaurant with tables on Piazza Navona or Campo de' Fiori is paying premium rent for the view and recovering it through your bill. The food is not the point. A €14 Aperol Spritz in these piazzas is not a scam — you're paying for the location. What is a scam is ordering food expecting it to be good. Sit for a drink if you want the atmosphere. Eat somewhere else.

The rule

In Rome, the more beautiful the piazza, the worse the food. The best meals are always on streets with no view at all.

Vatican area — Via della Conciliazione and surroundings

The Vatican corridor

The street leading to St Peter's Square is one of the most aggressively tourist-oriented dining corridors in Italy. Prices are 40–60% above the Roman average for equivalent food. The restaurants are not bad because they are lazy — they are bad because they don't need to be good. Cross the river to Prati for the same price and actual quality.

Florence

Ponte Vecchio surroundings — 100-metre radius

The bridge tax

Restaurants immediately around Ponte Vecchio charge a location premium with no quality justification. The further you walk from the bridge — especially into the Oltrarno neighbourhood on the south side — the better and cheaper the food gets. Ten minutes of walking is worth €8–12 per person.

Where to go instead

Walk south over the bridge, turn left, and keep walking for 8–10 minutes into the residential part of Oltrarno. The restaurants there serve Florentines, not tourists.

Venice

Anywhere within sight of St Mark's Square

The most expensive bad food in Italy

Venice has an undeserved reputation for bad, overpriced food. The reputation is accurate for the tourist corridor from the train station to St Mark's and back. It is entirely inaccurate for Cannaregio, Castello, and the residential parts of Dorsoduro. Two different cities operating under the same name.

Named: avoid

Restaurants on the Riva degli Schiavoni waterfront promenade — consistently documented as the highest price-to-quality ratio in Venice. The view costs approximately €12 per plate.

Naples

Piazza del Plebiscito surroundings

Tourist-facing pizzerias near the main square

Naples has the best pizza in the world and the worst tourist-facing pizzerias in Italy, sometimes within 50 metres of each other. Pizzerias with photos of Vesuvius on the menu, English translations before Italian, and a host outside waving at tourists: the pizza will be edible but it will not be what Naples is actually capable of. The city's best pizzerias have queues of locals and no street-facing promotion whatsoever.

The Neapolitan rule

If they're trying to get you in, don't go in. If there's a queue of Neapolitans outside at 1:00 PM with nowhere to sit, that's the place.

Tours not worth the price

Updated 2026

All cities

Hop-on hop-off buses

€25–35 per person for a recorded commentary on a double-decker bus. In Rome, the traffic means you spend more time stationary than moving. In Florence, the historic centre is largely closed to buses anyway. In Naples, the routes miss most of what matters. The only city where a similar concept makes partial sense is Sicily's Palermo, and there are better options there too. Use the money for a 2-hour walking tour with a local guide — you'll learn ten times more.

Rome

"Vatican by Night" and similar evening specials

The Vatican Museums offer occasional evening openings that are genuinely good — less crowded, different light in the galleries. What is not good value is the "exclusive evening experience" tour packages sold at €80–120 per person that bundle a mediocre dinner with the same evening opening ticket you could buy independently for €21. The experience is identical. You're paying €60–100 for a pasta dinner in a rented hall.

Rome

Catacombs "combo" tours sold near the Colosseum

Sellers near the Colosseum offer €45–65 combo tickets covering the Colosseum, Roman Forum, and the Catacombs as a single day package. The Catacombs are 7 kilometres from the Colosseum. This day will be exhausting, rushed, and you will remember nothing. The Catacombs deserve a dedicated half-day visit. Book them separately through the official site and do them on a different day.

Also note

Some combo tickets sold on the street do not include actual reserved entry slots for the Colosseum — they include a voucher that still requires queuing at the gate.

Amalfi Coast

Full-day coach tours from Naples or Sorrento

€65–95 per person for a day that involves 4 hours on a coach, 40 minutes in Positano, 40 minutes in Amalfi, a group lunch at a restaurant with a fixed tourist menu, and 4 more hours on a coach. The Amalfi Coast requires either a private car, the SITA bus (€2), or a boat to experience properly. Any format that puts 40 people on a schedule destroys the thing you came to see.

Tuscany

Chianti wine tours by minibus from Florence

The concept is sound. The execution, at scale, has become an assembly line. Many operators now run the same 3 wineries on the same route with the same script. The wineries on the standard routes are not the best wineries in Chianti — they are the wineries that can handle 200 tourists a day. If you want a real Chianti experience, rent a car, pick two small estates with appointment-only visits, and stay for lunch. The cost is lower and the memory is real.

Tourist traps & overrated experiences

Updated 2026

Rome

Tossing a coin in the Trevi Fountain

Not a trap — it's free and charming. What is a trap: the photographers who position themselves near the fountain and offer to take your photo for "a donation," the vendors selling overpriced Trevi-branded merchandise in the surrounding streets, and the ice cream shops on the immediate approach whose prices are 40–60% above the Rome average. The fountain itself is worth visiting. Everything within 50 metres of it is priced for people who just had an emotional moment and aren't thinking clearly.

Florence

Leather market stalls — San Lorenzo area

The outdoor market around San Lorenzo sells leather goods marketed as "genuine Florentine leather" at €20–80. Most of it is manufactured outside Italy. Vendors are aggressive and the "handmade" claims are largely fabricated. Genuine Florentine leather goods are available in the Oltrarno neighbourhood from workshops where you can watch the work being done — at similar or slightly higher prices for a product that is actually what it claims to be.

How to spot real Florentine leather

The artisan is physically present. There is a workshop behind or beside the shop. The item has no barcode sticker on the back. The vendor does not immediately offer a discount.

Venice

Murano glass sold outside Murano

The Murano glass sold in Venice's tourist shops — particularly around St Mark's and the Rialto — is almost entirely manufactured in China and imported. The genuine product is made on Murano island by licensed glassblowers and carries a Vetro Artistico® Murano trademark. If the shop is not on Murano and the item does not carry that specific trademark, it is not Murano glass regardless of what the label says.

All cities

Limoncello and olive oil "direct from the producer"

Shops selling limoncello, olive oil, and truffles "direct from the producer" in tourist areas of Rome, Florence, and Amalfi towns are rarely anything of the kind. The free tasting is a sales technique. The products are not necessarily bad — they are simply the same products you would find in a supermarket, sold at a 200–300% markup in a prettier bottle. Excellent olive oil and limoncello are available in any Italian supermarket at honest prices.

Naples & Pompeii

Cameo jewellery near Pompeii and on the Amalfi Coast

Cameo carving is a genuine Neapolitan craft tradition. The cameos sold in souvenir shops near Pompeii and in Amalfi Coast towns are overwhelmingly mass-produced in resin, not carved from shell. A hand-carved shell cameo costs €80–250 and is sold by the craftsperson directly or in certified shops. Anything under €30 is resin. The souvenir shops do not distinguish between the two.

Active scams — 2026

Verified Mar 2026

Rome

Termini Station area

Plainclothes police impersonation

Documented and active. Men show unofficial-looking IDs and ask to check your wallet for counterfeit bills. This is not a real police procedure. If approached, say "chiamo la polizia vera" (I'll call the real police) and walk toward the station. Real plainclothes officers in Italy do not perform wallet checks on tourists on the street.

Spanish Steps, Trevi Fountain

Friendship bracelet ambush

Men place a woven bracelet on your wrist before you can react, then demand €10–20 and become confrontational when refused. Keep hands in pockets or crossed in front of you when walking through these areas. If a bracelet is placed on you, remove it immediately and hand it back without engaging further.

Around major sights — Rome, Florence, Venice

Clipboard petition distraction

A person — often appearing to be a child or teenager — asks you to sign a petition for a charitable cause. While you are occupied, an accomplice picks your pocket. Active near the Duomo in Florence, around the Colosseum in Rome, and near the Rialto in Venice. Do not engage with anyone holding a clipboard near a tourist site. Say "no grazie" and keep walking.

Venice

Water taxi from Marco Polo Airport

Unlicensed water taxi drivers

Licensed water taxis from Marco Polo Airport to Venice cost €110–130 for up to 4 people — fixed and posted. Unlicensed drivers inside the arrivals hall offer the same trip for €70–80 and then add "landing fees," "night surcharge," and "luggage fees" once you are on the water. Use only taxis with the official yellow licence plate on the boat. Book in advance at motoscafivenezia.it or veneziaunica.it.

Naples

Street market areas — Porta Nolana, Via Toledo

Electronics and luxury goods at "incredible prices"

Sealed boxes of iPhones, AirPods, sunglasses, and handbags sold at 60–80% below retail. The boxes contain rocks, broken devices, or cheap replicas. This scam has been operating in Neapolitan markets for decades and the technique is unchanged. There is no legitimate reason a sealed iPhone costs €180. If you are shown a box and asked to decide quickly, the answer is always no.

Classic first-timer mistakes

Italy-wide

Trying to do Rome, Florence, Venice, and the Amalfi Coast in 10 days

This itinerary exists everywhere online and it will produce a trip of exhausted commuting with no actual experience of anything. Rome alone requires 4 days to see properly. The Amalfi Coast requires 2–3 days to be worth the journey. Choosing fewer places and staying longer is not a compromise — it is the correct way to travel in Italy. The guide's itineraries are built around this principle.

The rule

If your itinerary requires a different city every night, you are not experiencing Italy. You are ticking boxes on a list that will feel hollow within a week of returning home.

Booking accommodation inside the Venice historic centre for a first visit

Accommodation inside Venice's historic islands costs 40–70% more than equivalent quality on the Mestre mainland or in the Lido, a 10–15 minute journey away. For a first visit of 2–3 days, the price premium for waking up "inside Venice" is rarely justified by what you actually gain. The exception: if you are staying 4+ nights and have the budget — the experience of the city at 6 AM before the day-trippers arrive is genuinely different and worth paying for.

Renting a car and driving into historic city centres

Every major Italian historic city centre — Rome, Florence, Venice, Naples, Bologna, Siena — has a ZTL (Zona a Traffico Limitato). Driving into a ZTL without a permit triggers an automatic fine of €80–165 per violation, per camera. The cameras are not visible. Your rental company will charge an administrative fee on top of the fine. Never drive into a walled historic centre. Park at the edge, use public transport, or ask your hotel explicitly whether they have a permit arrangement.

ZTL signs to recognise

Round red circle with "ZTL" in white text, often with active/inactive hours posted below. If you see this sign, you have already crossed the boundary.

Standing at the bar vs sitting at a table — not knowing the price difference

In virtually every Italian bar and café, there are two prices: one for drinking at the counter (standing), one for table service. The difference is typically 50–100%. A €1.20 espresso at the bar becomes a €3.50 espresso if a waiter brings it to you at a table. Both are legal and posted by law on the price list inside. Neither is a scam. What is a problem is not knowing the difference and feeling overcharged when you were simply exercising a choice you didn't know you were making.

Buying train tickets on the platform the day of travel

High-speed trains between major Italian cities (Rome–Florence, Florence–Venice, Rome–Naples) have dynamic pricing identical to airlines. A ticket bought 3 weeks ahead costs €19–29. The same ticket bought at the station the morning of travel costs €59–89. There is no operational difference between the seats. The entire saving is in advance purchase. This guide's logistics sections include specific booking windows for each route.

Visiting major sights at midday in July and August

The Colosseum at 1:00 PM in August is 38°C with no shade and a 45-minute queue even with a reservation. The same site at 8:30 AM is 24°C, quiet, and dramatically more visually coherent — the early light is also better for photography. Every major outdoor site in Italy should be visited in the first 90 minutes after opening. This is not a preference. It is the difference between an experience and an endurance test.
