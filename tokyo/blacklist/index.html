The Black List
Scams · Tourist Traps · Honest Mistakes 2026
18 traps identified Verified August 2026
Tokyo is extraordinarily safe and honest — but that makes the handful of real traps more dangerous. Most tourists never encounter them. This list covers the ones that cost real money, ruin real plans, and target visitors who haven't been warned.
How to use this list Every entry here was independently verified. Nothing is sponsored. No tourism board or business paid to be included or excluded. We flag patterns — not isolated incidents — that have been consistently reported by travellers.

Safety & Transport Scams
3 entries
Transportation
Airports / Late Night / Shinjuku Station
Unlicensed Taxi Touts
Men approach at airports and late-night stations offering "cheap taxi" or "fast ride." Do not get in. These are unlicensed vehicles. In extreme cases this has led to robbery or assault (the "express kidnapping" pattern: take your card, force ATM withdrawals). Licensed Tokyo taxis are green, yellow, or black with illuminated signs — always find them at official ranks.
The ruleNever accept a ride offer from someone who approaches you. Walk to the taxi rank. The extra 5 minutes is not negotiable.
Nightlife
Kabukicho / Roppongi
Bar Touts Leading to Inflated Bills
Men approach outside Kabukicho or Roppongi bars and invite tourists "for a free drink." Once inside: minimum charges, mandatory food orders, or multiple drinks billed at ¥5,000–15,000 each. Bills for 2 people reaching ¥100,000+ have been reported. Tokyo Metropolitan Police report this pattern spreading beyond its traditional Kabukicho/Roppongi base into Shibuya and Ikebukuro, and have issued renewed public warnings about it through 2026. Nothing is free.
The ruleNever enter a bar with someone who approached you on the street. Walk in independently, ask for the menu and price list before ordering, and verify cover charges.
All Stations — After 00:30
The Last Train Trap
Tokyo trains stop around 00:00–01:00 depending on the line and direction. This is not a guideline — it is absolute. Miss the last train and your options are: wait until 05:00, take a taxi (¥3,000–8,000 depending on distance), or find a manga cafe. Many travellers lose significant money because they assumed "there would be another train."
Check in advanceUse the Navitime or HyperDia app to verify last train times for your exact route. Last train times vary by station and day.
Tourist Traps
5 entries
Third-Party Tickets
Ghibli Museum — Mitaka
Third-Party Resellers at 3–5× the Official Price
Ghibli Museum tickets sell exclusively through the official Lawson ticket system (lawsonticket.com) on the 10th of each month for the following month. They sell out in minutes. Third-party sites sell them at ¥5,000–12,000 (face value: ¥1,000 adult, down to ¥100 for young children). These resold tickets may be refused at the door.
The only waySet a calendar reminder for the 10th of the month at 10:00 Japan time. Use the official Lawson system. No shortcuts exist.
teamLab Borderless / Planets
Reseller Tickets for teamLab
teamLab sells out weeks ahead. Third-party tickets are sold at 2–4× the official price. The official site (teamlab.art) is the only legitimate source — note that Borderless uses dynamic pricing (roughly ¥3,600–¥5,600 adult), so compare against the current official price, not an old figure. Resold tickets have no refund or exchange — if refused at the door, the loss is total.
Book officialteamlab.art only. Book as soon as your travel dates are confirmed. No walk-in availability on busy days.
JR Pass
Everywhere — Pre-Departure Purchase
The JR Pass Math Problem
The 7-day JR Pass is ¥50,000 through September 2026, rising to ¥53,000 from October 1, 2026 (JR Group's confirmed nationwide increase — the 14-day and 21-day passes rise too). It's only worth it if you take multiple Shinkansen trips. For Tokyo-only trips: it is never worth it. Even Tokyo + one Kyoto round trip (roughly ¥28,000–29,000) doesn't cover the pass cost, and the gap only widens after the October increase. Calculate your actual routes before buying. Many travellers buy it out of anxiety and spend ¥50,000+ on a pass they use for ¥18,000 of travel.
Do the mathList every train you plan to take. Calculate individual fares at hyperdia.com. Only buy the pass if the total exceeds its price — and buy before October 2026 if your travel dates allow it.
Food and Restaurants
Senso-ji / Shibuya / Shinjuku Tourist Zones
Tourist-Facing Restaurants in Prime Locations
Any restaurant with photos on the menu, staff standing outside calling you in, or a prime view of Senso-ji / the Crossing is charging a location premium. A ramen bowl that costs ¥800 everywhere else costs ¥1,400–1,800 here. The quality difference is also real — these restaurants serve volume, not craft.
Two streets backWalk two streets behind any major attraction. The price drops, the quality rises, and the staff stop smiling at you to come in.
Tsukiji Outer Market
Premium "Tuna Auction" Tour Claims
The Tsukiji inner market moved to Toyosu in 2018. The tuna auction at Toyosu requires an official lottery application months in advance (free). Third-party "tuna auction tour" sellers charge ¥8,000–20,000 for viewings that may be of the outer market only, fish displays in restaurants, or simply a guided walk of publicly accessible areas.
The outer market is freeThe Tsukiji Outer Market is completely free to visit. Just walk in. Any paid "tour" of the outer market is overpriced.
Money & Payment Mistakes
4 entries
Cash & Cards
Everywhere — Cash Usage
Assuming Tokyo is Cashless Like Home
Japan is cash-forward, not cashless. Many small restaurants, shrines, some taxis, local shops, and traditional markets are cash only. Arriving without yen and depending on card acceptance is a common failure. 7-Eleven ATMs accept foreign cards reliably. Japan Post ATMs too. Carry ¥5,000–10,000 at all times.
Withdraw at 7-Eleven7-Eleven bank ATMs work with nearly all foreign cards. Available 24/7. The most reliable option in Japan.
Currency Exchange Counters
Airport Exchange at Narita / Haneda
Airport exchange counters offer rates 8–12% below mid-market. For ¥100,000 worth of exchange, that's ¥8,000–12,000 lost immediately on arrival. Use the 7-Eleven ATM inside the airport or in the city instead. Your home bank card with low foreign transaction fees will give a better rate.
ATM firstDon't exchange cash at the airport counter. Withdraw from the 7-Eleven ATM in the arrival hall instead.
IC Card (Suica/Pasmo)
Not Loading the IC Card Before Exploring
Suica and Pasmo IC cards work on every train, Metro, bus, and most convenience stores. Not having one means paying cash for every trip, queueing for tickets each time, and being unable to tap through gates. Load ¥3,000–5,000 immediately on arrival at any station machine. 2026 note: the Welcome Suica Mobile app (JR East, iOS-only, no Japanese bank card needed) is now the most reliable digital option; standard Apple/Google Wallet Suica top-up works for some foreign cards but is inconsistent — check before travel.
First thing at the stationBuy and load IC card before leaving the airport station. It eliminates all transit friction for the entire trip.
All Restaurants
Tipping — It Can Cause Offence
Tipping is not customary in Japan. In many settings it can cause genuine confusion or be politely refused. Service charges are not added to bills. The price you see is what you pay. Attempting to tip enthusiastically in a traditional restaurant can create an awkward interaction for everyone involved.
No tipping anywhereNot at restaurants, not at hotels, not in taxis. If exceptional service deserves acknowledgement, a sincere verbal thank-you (arigatou gozaimasu) is the correct response.
Planning & Booking Mistakes
6 entries
Advance Bookings
Shibuya Sky / Tokyo Skytree / teamLab
Arriving Without Pre-Booked Tickets
Shibuya Sky, teamLab, and the Ghibli Museum sell out advance slots, especially on weekends and during cherry blossom / golden week / autumn. Walk-in availability is not guaranteed. Travellers who arrive expecting to buy on the day often find nothing available — or pay resellers 2–3× the face value in desperation. Also note: Shibuya Sky's official site has rejected many foreign-issued cards since mid-2024 — if yours fails, book through a reputable reseller (e.g. Klook) instead.
Book before you boardShibuya Sky and teamLab: book at least 2 weeks ahead for weekends. Ghibli Museum: book the month before you travel via the Lawson system.
Tokyo Skytree
Climbing Tokyo Skytree Without Weighing the Alternatives
Skytree tickets cost ¥2,100–3,400 depending on deck and day, and require 90+ minutes including queuing and elevator time. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observation deck (45th floor) is free, has no queue to speak of, stays open until 23:00 (North Tower), and provides views that rival paid alternatives. Unless Skytree is specifically important to you, the Government Building is the rational choice.
Free alternative existsMetropolitan Government Building deck: free, 45th floor, North Tower until 23:00. Same category of view at zero cost.
Logistics
All Itineraries
Overloading Each Day
Tokyo visitors commonly plan 8–10 sights per day. A realistic Tokyo day accommodates 3–4 areas at depth. The distances look small on a map — 25 minutes from Asakusa to Shibuya, 20 minutes from Shibuya to Roppongi — but door-to-door including navigation, wayfinding, platform changes, and walking exits adds real time. Over-scheduled days consistently disappoint.
The sustainable numberPlan 3 areas per day maximum. Add one optional if you arrive early. Finish with energy for the evening.
Imperial Palace East Gardens / Many Museums
Monday Closures — The Recurring Problem
Many of Tokyo's most important institutions close on Mondays: Imperial Palace East Gardens (also closed Fridays), most major museums (Tokyo National, Mori Art, National Museum of Western Art), some specialty sites. Arriving on a Monday without checking closure schedules is a frequently repeated mistake — especially for travellers who have one free day in the city.
Check before planningVerify closure days for every attraction before building your itinerary. The guide notes closure days throughout — use them.
Airport Transfer Planning
Underestimating Narita Transfer Time
Narita Airport (NRT) is 60–90 minutes from central Tokyo by train — depending on which service and which area you're travelling to. Many travellers budget "an hour" and find themselves cutting flight margins fine. Allow 2.5–3 hours before international departure from Narita. Haneda (HND) is easier: 45–60 minutes to most central stations.
Narita bufferNarita: depart Tokyo 3–4 hours before flight. Never less. Haneda: 2–2.5 hours is adequate for most routes.
Japan Visa / ETA
Japan ETA / JESTA Confusion — There's No Live Requirement Yet
Despite what some third-party sites claim, Japan has not launched a pre-travel electronic travel authorization. The planned system, called JESTA, is targeted for fiscal 2028 (April 2028 at the earliest) — as of August 2026 no application process, portal, or fee exists. Visa-exempt passport holders enter Japan under the current rules with just a valid passport (typically up to 90 days for tourism). Sites charging money for a "Japan ETA" application in 2026 are exploiting this confusion — do not pay them. This is not the airline's problem if you fall for it. It is yours.
Verify at the sourceCheck current entry requirements only at official pages (mofa.go.jp or japan.travel) — never at a third-party site charging an "ETA processing fee." If your nationality isn't on Japan's visa-exempt list, apply for a standard visa or the Japan eVisa well ahead of travel.
