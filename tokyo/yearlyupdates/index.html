Entry requirements — Japan ETA
Japan ETA now required for some nationalities — check before booking flights
Japan introduced an Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) system for certain passport holders. The requirement varies by nationality. The ETA is applied for online, is inexpensive, and typically approved quickly — but travellers who discover this at check-in face boarding denial. Verify your nationality's requirements at japan.travel/en/plan/eta before purchasing any flights. Apply at least 72 hours before departure.
Suica / IC Card — 2026 status
IC Cards — Physical and Digital
The situation regarding IC cards for foreign visitors improved in 2025–2026. Digital Suica can now be added to Apple Wallet or Google Pay for many foreign bank-issued cards (Visa, Mastercard) — check your banking app before departure. Physical Suica and Pasmo cards are available at all major station machines. The ¥500 card deposit is refundable when you return the physical card before leaving Japan (Suica) or at Pasmo counters.
Physical only for foreign visitors (prev. years) → Digital Suica now available for many foreign cards (2026)
Prices — major attractions
Shibuya Sky — Shibuya Scramble Square
Shibuya Sky admission is ¥2,000 adults (unchanged from 2025). Book online at shibuyasky.com — sunset and weekend slots sell out. Walk-in availability on weekday mornings is occasionally possible but not reliable. The outdoor terrace is the key experience; factor weather into your timing.
¥2,000 (confirmed unchanged) → ¥2,000 adult (2026) shibuyasky.com
Tokyo Skytree
Skytree prices increased in 2023 and have remained stable. Tembo Deck (350m): ¥2,100 (weekday) to ¥2,400 (weekend). Tembo Galleria (450m): additional ¥1,000. Advance online booking is available and avoids the worst queues. Reminder: the Metropolitan Government Building (free, 45th floor) provides comparable views with no queuing and no cost.
¥2,060 (pre-2023) → ¥2,100–2,400 (2026) tokyo-skytree.jp
Tokyo National Museum — Ueno
Tokyo National Museum now uses a timed-entry system for busy periods (cherry blossom season, Golden Week, autumn leaves). General admission: ¥1,000 adults. Special exhibitions require separate tickets purchased in advance — check tnm.jp before visiting. Monday closures remain in effect.
¥620 (pre-2023 general) → ¥1,000 adult general (2026) tnm.jp

New Tips for 2026
Added 2026
Azabudai Hills — new development
Azabudai Hills — Minato Ward (opened 2023)
Azabudai Hills is Tokyo's largest recent development: a mixed-use tower complex containing teamLab Borderless, galleries, restaurants, the Mori JP Tower observation floor, and a significant green public plaza. Worth a separate visit beyond teamLab. The complex connects to Roppongi Hills via a 15-minute walk. Nearest Metro: Kamiyacho (Hibiya line).
IC card — digital options
Suica via Apple / Google Wallet
Foreign visitors can now add Suica to Apple Wallet or Google Pay from supported countries using compatible Visa or Mastercard cards. Check your banking app before travel — not all foreign-issued cards are eligible yet. When it works, this eliminates the need for a physical IC card and allows top-up directly from your phone. Still carry ¥5,000–10,000 in cash as backup.
Practical
Luggage forwarding — takuhaibin
Takuhaibin (door-to-door luggage delivery) is one of Tokyo's most under-used advantages. Send your luggage from your hotel to any other hotel, airport, or even to a convenience store near a train station the night before check-out. Cost: ¥1,500–2,500 per bag. Services: Yamato Transport (Kuroneko), Sagawa. Most hotels can arrange it. This transforms day trips and airport days completely.
Apps That Actually Work in Tokyo (2026)
Google Maps now handles Tokyo transit accurately including platform-level directions. Navitime is better for last-train queries and provides actual walking times. TableCheck / Ikyu for restaurant reservations at mid-to-high range restaurants (some require Japanese phone number — use hotel concierge as intermediary). Tabelog for ratings and discovery (all in Japanese, but numbers translate).
Honest money notes
Daily Budget Reality — Early 2026
At ¥150/USD (early 2026 rates), realistic daily costs: Budget traveller: ¥8,000–12,000/day (hostel, convenience store breakfast, ramen/gyudon meals, metro travel, one paid attraction). Mid-range: ¥15,000–22,000/day (3-star hotel, varied restaurants, 1–2 paid attractions, comfortable pace). Comfortable: ¥25,000–40,000/day adds a nicer dinner and taxi use. Accommodation is the largest variable — book 2–4 months ahead for best rates, especially during cherry blossom season.
Seasonal Alerts
Year-round planning
Cherry blossom — late March to early April
Cherry blossom timing: unpredictable until 6 weeks before bloom
Tokyo's cherry blossom season typically peaks late March to early April, but exact dates shift based on winter temperatures. Full bloom might be March 24 or April 5 in different years. Reliable forecasts only emerge 4–6 weeks before bloom (Japan Meteorological Corporation publishes these). If you book flights far in advance around late March, you're making an educated bet — not a guarantee. Peak bloom lasts 5–10 days, and crowds at Ueno Park, Shinjuku Gyoen, Chidorigafuchi, and Yoyogi Park are intense. Accommodation prices increase significantly during bloom weeks.
Golden Week — late April to early May
Golden Week: Japan's busiest domestic travel period
Golden Week (April 29 – May 5) is Japan's major national holiday cluster. Domestic travel spikes dramatically: Shinkansen books out weeks in advance, popular attractions have maximum crowds, hotel prices increase 30–50%. If you travel during Golden Week, book all accommodation and Shinkansen at least 6–8 weeks ahead. Expect queues at Tsukiji, Senso-ji, Shibuya, and Harajuku to be the longest of the year. The advantage: the city is festive, events happen throughout, and Golden Gai and Omoide Yokocho are particularly atmospheric.
Summer — July to August
Summer Heat — July and August
Tokyo summer is genuinely challenging: temperatures 30–36°C with high humidity. Morning sightseeing (before 10:00) and evening sightseeing (after 18:00) are the functional windows. Midday is for air-conditioned museums, department store food halls, or the well-air-conditioned underground shopping passages beneath Tokyo Station and Shinjuku. Hydration is critical — convenience stores and vending machines everywhere, heat stroke is a real risk for visitors unaccustomed to this humidity level.
Summer Fireworks Festivals
Sumida River Fireworks Festival (last Saturday of July, 2026 TBC) is one of the largest fireworks displays in Japan. Crowds are extreme — entire areas near Asakusa and Sumida River become impassable. Viewing spots fill 3–4 hours before the event. If attending, commit to it fully and plan your entire day around it. Do not casually "swing by."
Autumn — October to November
Autumn Leaves — Koyo Season
October to mid-December brings koyo (autumn foliage) — Tokyo's second most beautiful season. Shinjuku Gyoen, Ueno Park, Yoyogi Park, and Rikugien Garden turn spectacular. Crowds are large but less intense than cherry blossom season. The key parks charge admission but remain well worth it. Rikugien in Bunkyo ward is particularly photogenic and often overlooked by first-time visitors.
